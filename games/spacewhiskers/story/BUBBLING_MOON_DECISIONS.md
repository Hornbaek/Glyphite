## This world (Chapter 9, "Worlds out of balance")

- **Decision:** The order is fixed, not chosen by the player. It keeps the character arc and the fading Milky Way clean.
- **Decision:** The order is Cheese Forest, Bubbling Moon, Sleeping Station, then Scrap Belt. The last two are swapped from STORY_V2 so Pip patching Miss Clou's suit lands right before Ch 10.
- **Decision:** The worlds form a trail. Each one hands the next a reason to go. The Cheese Forest tower's clues point to the Bubbling Moon, whose pumping station points on to the Sleeping Station, which leads to the Scrap Belt. Miss Clou knows where the moon is but did not know it mattered until the tower said so. The fortress's location is already known, and what is missing is how to get in.
- **Decision:** The Bubbling Moon is strange and playful in a creative way, though the universe is already sad. Giant cheese craters with Swiss-cheese holes are pits, and bubbles of old milk pop up as bouncy platforms. A big wobbling curd drifts across the dry lake like a slow boat. The dried lakes form a glossy white crust that mirrors the sky.
- **Decision:** The old pumping station holds the pipe map, reused in Ch 12's milk works. It was built to pump milk from these lakes to the fortress, so MEGA-MJAU's empire began by draining this moon.
- **Decision:** The moon gives three rewards. They are the pipe map as a scrapbook page, a scrapbook photo with history from the station (an old plaque or a faded photo of the first pumping day), and a pump housing that Pip bolts on as a fuel tank, so the ship can run farther.
- **Decision:** The moon's first photo is the mirror-flat reflection of the thin Milky Way underfoot. This is a placeholder and may change.
- **Open:** Hooks and first photos for the Bubbling Moon, Sleeping Station and Scrap Belt, so the middle is more than travel. *(`BUBBLING_MOON.md` proposes a hook for the moon: it has hiccups.)*
- **Open:** What Pip and Miss Clou each change in, world by world, from STORY_V2's list: she shares what she knows, takes the dangerous jobs and admits she has been selfish; he sees her care for the freed cats.
- Related: "Ch 9: STORY_V2's four pieces become a trail with each world pointing to the next, and the delivery schedule needs a use in Ch 12." and "Ch 12: rewrite for the stealth rules, with the patrol routes guiding Pip to Miss Clou, and keep the pipe map from the Bubbling Moon in the milk works."

## Every cat site, and the tone

- **Decision:** Every cat site is high tech but half finished. The tone is silly, not scary, because it is a children's game.
- **Keep:** Humour and sincerity both stay. The red dot, the tractor and the capital letters are the tone, and Act IV is where it turns sincere.
- **Keep:** The message stays about its own world: mice, cats, learned fear, shared work. The game should not preach about real-world systems.
- **Open:** Each world in Ch 5 to 9 needs its own hook so the middle is more than travel. Ch 5 and 6 have one now (half-finished tech); Ch 9 does not.

## Pip and Miss Clou

- **Decision:** Pip is naive, a bit daft, very kind and eager to help. His stubbornness drives the story from the first page.
- **Decision:** When something catches Miss Clou off guard she follows one pattern: a visible startle, a quick snap back to her cool, mysterious facade, then desperation leaking through. It appears in Ch 2, 4 and 6.
- **Decision:** Miss Clou is lazy. She lets Pip drive and do the legwork, and leaves her own projects behind like every cat.
- **Decision:** Miss Clou has a slow, jazzy music theme whenever she talks or the player interacts with her.
- **Idea:** The jazz starts cool and controlled, then loosens as she warms to Pip, with a note that wobbles when her desperation leaks through.
- **Decision (Ch 4):** Her using him starts on the false cheese moon. Being lazy, she lets him do the legwork while she hovers, hints and turns up with help, as STORY_V2 describes from Ch 5. She never tells him what she is after.
- **Decision (Ch 5):** Miss Clou hitches a ride in Pip's rocket because she is lazy; her own rocket stays on the moon.
- **Decision (Ch 8):** Cat parts are allowed on the bigger ship, but Pip must build it, not Miss Clou. He does not connect electronics or systems. He finds, for example, a huge metal piece, bolts it on as hull and never uses its high tech. The ship handles more in space but is still primitive. Miss Clou knows she would instinctively wire everything up, which MEGA-MJAU would spot. This rule replaces STORY_V2's "no cat parts allowed" for the whole game.
- **Idea:** Her habit of leaving things unfinished pays off at the end, when she is the one who stays and finishes something: the farm, her crew, the apology.

## The scrapbook (Pip's instant camera)

- **Decision:** Pip keeps a scrapbook with an instant camera. He photographs things he sees along the way, and the ancient-history fragments about the Holy Cow, and about cats and mice living together, are collected in all worlds.
- **Decision:** The book is automated. Photos are added automatically and the player never places them. The player can always look at it, and it is brought up in situations, for example Miss Clou saying "didn't you take a photo of the code?"
- **Decision:** A micro-interaction replaces manual layout. When something is worth capturing, a prompt appears and the player presses a button to make Pip take the photo. It also hints that the thing matters.
- **Decision:** The scrapbook has a function along the way, not only a reward at the end. Pip makes it for fun, but it becomes a history book the whole society can use to remember the lost history. It has five uses, all to be built: proof (showing a page changes how people treat Pip), key (a photo in one world unlocks something in another), gift (for example a photo of her old crew moves Miss Clou), map (pages hint where to go next), and finale evidence against MEGA-MJAU that cats and mice once cooperated.
- **Decision:** Pip loves taking pictures and noting things down on the journeys. Miss Clou disdains it ("why do you keep doing this, it's ridiculous").
- **Idea:** The player nudges a small frame or picks between two or three framings. It depends on the engine, so not sure it is possible.
- **Idea:** The book echoes Grandma Brie's picture book from the opening, with its torn page, and could fill the missing page at the Ch 16 mural.
- **Open:** The finale use of the scrapbook against MEGA-MJAU needs more work.

## The Cheese Forest, the world before it (for the trail and the tone)

- The Cheese Forest is quite sad, so the player feels that the Holy Cow is missing: dry trees, hard grey fallen fruit and a thin sky. One tree still has a single small cheese fruit, and Pip's reaction to it carries the hope for the rest of the game.
- Its gameplay is the platform climb up the trees to the old lookout tower, no help from locals; at the top, the door code, fragments of ancient history, and something to upgrade the ship with.
- **Idea:** The carvings show the cow with a mouse and a cat beside her, worn and half hidden, as an early hint of the Ch 16 mural. Miss Clou goes quiet for a second, then brushes it off.

## The Sleeping Station, the world after it (so the two do not repeat each other)

- The Sleeping Station is quiet and strange, gentle and not scary. Machines loyally do their jobs with nobody there: a cleaning robot polishing the same spot, an elevator opening for nobody, an announcement system cheerfully calling departures to empty platforms. It ties to cats abandoning things while the machines were never told to stop.
