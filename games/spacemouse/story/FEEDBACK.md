# Star Whiskers: The Holy Cow — feedback on the story

Notes on [STORY.md](STORY.md) (the English version of the owner's Swedish documents) and the cover
(`../ref/cover_star_whiskers.png`). **The owner agreed to all of them on 2026-11-12**; STORY.md is still the original and gets them when the story is rewritten.
The prompt for an updated cover is in [COVER_PROMPT.md](COVER_PROMPT.md).

---

## What already works

- **The theme is in the mechanics.** "Cooperation beats control" is acted out each time the player frees a cat by
  breaking its control device, instead of only being said in a cutscene. Keep that link; it's the best idea here.
- **The abandoned stations have a reason to exist.** Cats get bored and leave their projects, so space is full of
  half-finished stations with confused security systems. That one character trait explains the levels, the loot and
  the humour.
- **The inventor cat has a real arc:** pride and secrecy, then a confession, then making amends. A villain's maker who
  turns helper is a strong engine for a story.
- **The bottle rocket.** It is funny, it's what the cover and the build system are about, and children could build one
  themselves.
- **The villain's logic is easy to follow.** "Whoever controls the milk controls the future" and "to it, control is the
  same as order" are clear to a seven-year-old and still say something.
- **The title.** *Star Whiskers* fits both heroes (a mouse and a cat both have whiskers), and "Holy cow!" is already
  something people shout in English, so the subtitle is a joke too.

## What could be stronger

### 1. The mouse has no flaw
"Doesn't give up" is a strength, so the mouse starts out already fine and never has to change. The cat changes
and the mouse goes along with it. Some ways to fix that, from small to larger:
- **The mouse distrusts cats.** The story hints at this ("the cat isn't evil just because it is a cat"). Put it in
  Act I: the town is wary of the cat at the edge of town, and so is the mouse. The friendship then changes both of
  them.
- **A small greed test in Chapter 1.** The mouse finds the last hidden wedge of cheese. It can eat it, or share it with
  the old mouse who tells the legend. The theme then starts with the player's own choice, and the old mouse can bring
  it up at the end.

### 2. Why does the cat need a *mouse*?
Cats have better technology and have flown for ages, so why does this cat send a small mouse in a plastic bottle?
**Suggestion: the robot's defences are built to detect cat technology.** The cat designed them, so it knows. Any cat
ship is spotted at once. A ship made of bottles, tins and tape doesn't show up on its scanners at all. That:
- explains why the cat hands over the blueprint in Chapter 2 and still keeps its distance,
- makes building with junk *matter* in the plot, not only in the gameplay,
- pays off in Chapter 11: the last ship gets through the defences *because* it is made of junk. The mouse's
  improvising beats the cat's engineering, and they need both.

### 3. Pick one rule for milk
At the moment the Holy Cow's milk feeds the cheese plants through roots. Cats also drink milk, and some moons have
milk lakes. So where does the cats' milk come from, and why does the robot need *this* cow? One simple rule fixes it:
**the Holy Cow is the source of all milk in the universe, and the trail of her milk across the sky is the Milky Way.**
Cheese grows where it reaches, and the milk lakes fill from it. If the robot holds her, it holds everything. Children
get the pun, the chapter in the bubbling moon makes sense, and the night sky can show the Milky Way fading level by
level, which works as a progress bar painted on the background.

### 4. Act II–III repeats its clues
"Someone moved a lot of energy somewhere far away" turns up in Chapter 4, Chapter 5 and Chapter 10, and the hidden cats
in Chapter 8 already know where the fortress is. **Give each world one new piece of the puzzle:** *what* happened (the
moon, Ch. 4), *who* (the recording, Ch. 6), *where* (the shipyard cats, Ch. 8), *how to get in* (the worlds in Ch. 9,
one part each), *its weak spot* (Ch. 10). Show the pieces on a clue board in the ship, so the player sees the case
come together.

### 5. The story has no low point
After the confession the story only goes up. A low point just before the end would make the finale count:
**at the end of Act III the robot captures the inventor cat and puts a control device on it.** In the control centre
(Ch. 13), the mouse has to free its own friend by doing what it has done all game: jump on the device. The core
mechanic then carries the emotional turning point. The cat, freed by the mouse it once looked down on, can be the one
to switch off the signal.
(A smaller version: the robot wrecks the bottle rocket on the way, which explains why they build a last ship in Ch. 11.)

### 6. The cow does nothing
She is the title character, but she is just carried around. The cover gives her a halo, so give her a character to
match:
- **Calm and unbothered**, as if she has seen a billion years go by. She says "Moo" at the worst possible moments,
  and everybody falls silent.
- **She helps in the escape.** In Chapter 16 the player runs out of the collapsing complex, and cheese and plants grow
  wherever her milk drips: new platforms grow ahead of you as you run. It's a level where the rescue *looks* like it
  is working.
- Keep it gentle: she has been held, not harmed. She is bored and homesick.

### 7. What happens to the robot?
"It collapses, not necessarily destroyed" is the right instinct for this audience. Go one step further. **The robot
is reset and gets a new job:** it ends up on the farm, a huge mechanical cat milking very gently and pulling the
plough. Its "control is order" logic becomes a joke ("THE CARROTS ARE IN PERFECT ROWS"). It fits the theme: the robot
was also stuck on one goal, and it is freed too. It could also be the "bigger ship" in the epilogue.
**Its weak spot** (still an open question in the concept) should be something only its maker knows. Two ideas,
both cat jokes: a test **red dot** the cat built in and forgot, which the robot can't help chasing; or the fact that
it **can't stand being ignored**, so the freed cats beat it by turning their backs on it, all together.

### 8. The farm contradicts itself
In Chapter 16 the cow goes home to her own planet; in Chapter 17 she lives on a farm with the mouse and the cat. It
can't be both. **Suggestion:** the farm *is* on her planet, and the mouse, the cat and the freed cats become her new
keepers. Pair it with a find in the temple ruins: **the old civilisation that looked after the cow was mice and cats
together.** The ending doesn't invent something new, it brings back something that once was, and the theme becomes
the world's history. The ruins can show it in pictures long before the player understands what they mean.

### 9. Plant the cat's friends early
Ch. 13 ("the cat meets old friends it thought it had lost") comes out of nowhere at the moment. Plant it: a photo
of the cat's old crew is pinned up in the shed (Ch. 2); the first cat freed in Ch. 5 is one of them, and the inventor
cat goes quiet. In Ch. 13 the player recognises the faces.

## The cover against the story

- **The mouse wears no space suit on the cover** (a red scarf and an adventurer's outfit), but the concept and the
  current game have Pip in a suit with a glass helmet. Either works: the scarf and outfit for the planets and the
  title screen, the helmet over them in space. Decide before new sprites are drawn.
- **The controlled cats on the cover look like an army of robots** (armoured, red-eyed). The story says they are
  ordinary cats with a device on their heads, and the "free, don't fight" idea depends on children seeing that. In
  the game, make the device obvious (a little antenna helmet with a red light) and make the freed cat look plainly
  like a cat again, so it reads as a cat being *released*.
- **The great robot cat's fortress with a cat's head** matches Chapter 12's "mechanical castle" well. The robot
  itself is the fortress's head on the cover; it could be literal: in the finale you climb the castle, and the castle
  *is* the robot.
- The title on the cover is *Star Whiskers*; the documents say *Space Mice* (*Rymdmöss*). Settle on one for the game
  page.

## Scope: what to build first

The whole story is large: 17 chapters, two playable characters, ships, vehicles, stations and generated levels. In
this engine, a good first playable is **Act I (Chapters 1–4)**:
1. the home town (a hub with the empty shop, the old mouse, a legend told in picture panels),
2. the cat's shed (the blueprint, the photo),
3. building the bottle rocket on a simple screen with slots for parts (body, cockpit, engine, tape),
4. a short flight, then the false cheese moon (a platforming level on the existing station tech, with the logbook
   and the first blueprint).

That tests the tone, the building screen and the story-by-objects idea before the big systems (playable cat, vehicles,
generated levels) are built. The current game's sectors (Cargo Bay, Hydroponics, Observation Deck) fit naturally as
Chapter 5, "The station nobody cleaned".
