AN AI-FIRST ASCII GAME ENGINE

Games drawn in 95 characters,
made by talking to Claude.

Glyphite is a game engine for the browser that draws everything with the printable ASCII characters, each in its own colour. Under the letters runs a real simulation: rock that breaks where it is hit, sand that pours and piles, light that casts shadows. And its games are not written by hand: they are made, and changed, in conversations with Claude Code.

Not a video: this is the engine, running in this page. Every character is a cell of rock, sand or light. Click or tap to drop a meteor; whatever loses its support breaks off and falls.

01 What it can do

A world you can break

Every cell is a material: rock, ice, glass, cheese. A blast carves it, and anything left without support breaks off as a rigid chunk that falls, tumbles, chips and settles back into the world.

Sand that flows

Sand, dust and rubble pour, pile and slide in a cellular simulation. Debris thrown by a blast becomes ground again where it lands.

Light, in text

Sun shadows, lamps that flicker and buzz, ambient occlusion, glowing materials and bloom, all worked out character by character, with eyes that adapt to the dark.

Physics underneath

Rigid bodies with contacts and friction, and a platformer controller with coyote time, jump buffering, wall jumps and floors you can jump up through.

Pixel art becomes ASCII

Art is cut from image sheets and turned into sprites whose characters follow its shapes, rotated and lit by the scene. Every original drawing is kept beside the game.

Fire, sparks and smoke

Particles, shock rings, flashes, screen shake and slow motion, and an NES-style sound chip that plays music and makes the booms.

One page per game

Each game is a single self-contained web page with nothing to install: WebGL first, a 2D canvas when there is no GPU, and detail from one to three characters per metre.

Built for AI sessions

One documented API, a starter game, a test hook every game answers, and checks that prove a level can be finished, so a Claude session can build and verify a game on its own.

02 How a game gets made

  1. Describe it. A few lines: who you play, the goal, the look. You play a space mouse in an abandoned space station, looking for cheese.
  2. Claude builds it. In a Claude Code session on the repository: the code, the levels, the art, and tests that play it.
  3. Play it and tune it. Every game opens in a web editor: turn its numbers, edit its code with a live preview, paint its levels in a pixel editor, see the art it was made from (behind the login, for now).
  4. Hand it back. The editor turns your changes into a prompt and a patch for the next session. Merging it deploys the site. The repository is the source of truth.

03 The games

Work in progress. These are test games, made to try out the engine as it grows. Expect rough edges, unfinished levels and things that change from one day to the next. Play them in the browser, no login needed: a keyboard works best.

04 The goal

A game engine made for one kind of picture, ASCII, and for one way of working: describing what you want and playing the result. Not just one game, but any 2D game: landers, platformers, falling-sand toys, top-down adventures. Physics and light come first, every game stays a single page, and the whole loop, from idea to deployed game, runs through AI sessions.

What comes next is picked from an idea bank of over a thousand ideas, from climbing and moving platforms to whole new games.
