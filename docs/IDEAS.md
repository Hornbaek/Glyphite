# Idea bank

Every feature Glyphite could have, from one-glyph polish to moonshots: 1133 ideas in 17 categories.
Generated 2026-10-15 by a multi-agent brainstorm (14 angles, curated, gap-hunted, then de-duplicated). Nothing here is planned or
promised: `docs/ROADMAP.md` is the plan. Use this as a quarry: pick an idea, check it against `CLAUDE.md`
(engine vs game, ASCII only, both renderers), and build it in a session.

Size: tiny · small · medium · large · moonshot. Wildness: practical · bold · crazy.

## Rendering & visual style

- **Animated material glyph cycles and palette cycling** (tiny, practical): Generalise the `crystal` style: any material can declare a glyph sequence, speed and spatial phase (lava `~=~-`, water `~-_-`, plasma `*+x+`), plus Mark Ferrari-style colour cycling. Liquids and hot rock come alive at no simulation cost.
- **Blue-noise dithering of glyph ramps** (tiny, practical): Replace the per-cell random byte in ramp selection with a world-anchored blue-noise texture, optionally offset over time. Rock faces get smoother gradients and no visible pattern while the camera scrolls.
- **Cel-shaded light bands** (tiny, practical): Quantise each cell's light into 3 or 4 bands before tone mapping, for a graphic-novel look with crisp terminator lines. It would be a toggle among the editor's tunables.
- **Chromatic aberration from the screen centre** (tiny, practical): Sample the atlas with red and blue pushed a pixel or two outward from the screen centre, scaled by blast flash or speed. It gives a cheap lens feel in the shader alone.
- **Crosshair styles with recoil spread** (tiny, practical): The mouse-aim cursor is drawn in HUD cells in styles like `+`, `[ ]` or `-( )-` that open up with recoil or inaccuracy.
- **Emoticon heads: mood shown as ASCII faces** (tiny, practical): NPC faces are drawn as `:)`, `>:(`, `o_O` or `x_x` and change with a mood value, so an NPC's state reads at a glance in a world made of characters.
- **Frosted HUD panels and text shadows** (tiny, practical): `hudBox` can dim and desaturate the world behind it (and blur it in GL) instead of filling solid black. HUD glyphs get an optional one-pixel dark shadow so they stay legible over bright explosions.
- **Glyph line drawing for limbs and ropes** (tiny, practical): `drawGlyphLine(x0, y0, x1, y1, col)` steps along a segment and picks `- \ | / _` for each cell from the slope and the 1:2 cell aspect. It covers limbs, ropes, tentacles, laser sights and chains, lit like terrain and built on `slopeGlyph`.
- **Grass and banners sway in wind** (tiny, practical): Grass-top cells and cloth sprites cycle through `|`, `/` and `\` with a phase taken from the local wind, so a gust visibly rolls across a meadow.
- **Heat shimmer over hot ground and lava** (tiny, practical): Shimmer now comes only from hot gas and fire particles. Feeding the heat field into the shimmer texture as well makes the air wobble over lava, glowing blast rims and rocket-scorched rock, in both renderers.
- **Low-health glyph vignette** (tiny, practical): The screen edges fill with red `%` and `#` that pulse with a heartbeat when the player is hurt or low on fuel, and recede as they recover.
- **Odometer-rolling HUD digits** (tiny, practical): Give HUD cells a vertical sub-cell offset so changing digits roll up like a mechanical counter, for score, fuel and altitude.
- **Off-screen target arrows** (tiny, practical): Pin `>`, `<`, `^`, `v` (and `/`, `\` for diagonals) to the screen edge, pointing at off-screen pads, pickups or enemies, with the distance in metres beside each one.
- **OKLab gradients and colour helpers** (tiny, practical): `mixOK(a, b, t)`, `oklch(l, c, h)` and gradient helpers for sky painters, HUD bars and heat colours, so mid-tones stay vivid instead of muddy and brightness steps look even.
- **Per-material incandescence colour curves** (tiny, practical): Every hot cell now glows along the same red-to-white curve. Let materials declare their own curve (copper flaring green, plasma going blue-white, slag staying dull red) and a crust glyph such as `%` that darkens as the cell cools.
- **Slow-motion colour drain and ghosting** (tiny, practical): While `FX.slow` is active, desaturate the world a little and turn on short phosphor ghosting, so slow motion looks different as well as moving slower.
- **Speed lines at high velocity** (tiny, practical): Above a speed threshold, faint `-` and `/` streaks rush past near the screen edges in the direction of travel, in anime style.
- **Sprite palette swaps and hit flashes** (tiny, practical): `drawSprite(..., { palette, flash, tint })` remaps a sprite's colours for team colours, white hit flashes, frozen blue or burning red, without duplicating the sprite data.
- **Sub-cell particle placement by glyph choice** (tiny, practical): A grain or spark in the top third of its cell draws as `'`, in the middle as `-` and at the bottom as `.` or `_`, and a backtick or a comma shifts it left or right. That roughly triples the apparent vertical resolution of sand, rain and sparks at no simulation cost.
- **ASCII autotiler for walls, pipes and frames** (small, practical): `autotile(mask, style)` picks the join glyph from a 4- or 8-neighbour mask in styles such as `+-|`, `.-'`, `/=\`, `#=#` or `[ ]`. One helper covers HUD boxes, pipes, rails, circuit boards and roguelike walls.
- **ASCII sextants: 2x3 sub-cell edge glyphs** (small, practical): Precompute the best printable glyph for each of the 64 on/off patterns of a 2x3 sub-cell grid, measured from the atlas's own pixels, then sample terrain occupancy at 2x3 per cell. Silhouettes get `/`, `_`, `'`, `.` and `L`-like edges instead of whole-cell steps, which gives about six times the edge detail without raising RES. *Unicode has sextant blocks for this. Glyphite can do the same trick with only the 95 printable characters.*
- **Auto-calibrated luminance ramps per font** (small, practical): A build tool, with a runtime fallback, that sorts each material's candidate glyphs by their measured coverage in the current font and evens out the steps. A ramp like ` .:;=+*#%@` then looks perceptually even in JetBrains Mono, VT323 or a bitmap VGA font.
- **Camera rigs and genre presets** (small, practical): Ready-made camera targets a game can return: a platformer dead zone, velocity look-ahead, a pull toward points of interest, Zelda-style screen-by-screen rooms, autoscroll, and multi-target framing that zooms out to keep both artillery teams in view.
- **Cinematic letterbox and camera rails** (small, practical): `cinematic([{x, y, t, ease}, ...])` drives the camera along keyframes while letterbox bars slide in from the HUD grid. Use it for intros, level fly-overs and crash replays.
- **Classic terminal and console palette presets** (small, practical): One-switch looks: green P1 phosphor, amber P3, white P4, CGA magenta and cyan, C64, PICO-8 and Game Boy greens, with colours snapped to the palette after lighting. The phosphor presets, with boosted glyph contrast, double as a high-contrast mode for low vision.
- **Crack glyphs show damage before collapse** (small, practical): Accumulate blast and impact damage per cell and swap its ramp glyph for crack glyphs (`;`, `%`, or `/` and `\` along the stress line) before the support check breaks it. Players can see a roof that is about to come down.
- **Decode, iris, melt and ramp-fade transitions** (small, practical): A transition library: a Matrix-style decode where cells scramble through random glyphs before settling, an iris wipe with an `O` rim, the DOOM column melt, and a fade that walks each cell down its ramp (`#`, `+`, `.`, blank) instead of dimming the colour.
- **Double-wide cells for top-down games** (small, practical): A layout mode that draws each world cell as two glyphs side by side (`##`, `[]`, `@ `) so cells come out square, as in classic roguelikes. It is useful once gravity can be zero or sideways.
- **FIGlet-style big text in the HUD** (small, practical): Bundle a few big ASCII fonts (block `#`, slant, outline, a 3x5 mini) and add `hudBig(text, col, row, font, colour)` for titles, LANDED, GAME OVER and level names, with optional wave or shake.
- **HUD sparklines and needle dials** (small, practical): `hud.plot(series)` draws sparklines of velocity or altitude history with `_`, `.`, `-` and `'` as four heights per cell, alongside bar gauges, tables and round dials whose needle is drawn with `-`, `\`, `|` and `/`. Cockpit telemetry, racing stats, city and science sims, and debugging views all get instrument panels from one kit.
- **Neon halos for emissive glyphs** (small, practical): Emissive materials and HUD text can turn on a soft SDF halo in their own colour with a flicker pattern, for neon signs, plasma conduits and holograms. The 2D path draws it with shadowBlur on a cached layer.
- **Opening crawl in perspective ASCII** (small, practical): A hudCrawl(text) helper scrolls intro text away into the distance. Rows shrink by stepping to thinner glyph ramps and fade into the starfield.
- **Outline pass for sprites and chunks** (small, practical): Optionally ring sprites, pickups and loose chunks with a one-cell outline, dark or in a team colour, with join glyphs picked from the neighbour mask. The lander then never dissolves into a busy rock face.
- **Per-material typeface in one atlas** (small, practical): Let each material pick a font family for its glyphs (wood in a slab serif, metal in a condensed mono, ice in a thin face), packed into one multi-font atlas. Materials then stay readable apart even in monochrome.
- **Photo mode with high-resolution glyph export** (small, practical): Freeze the simulation, fly a free camera, hide the HUD, drag the sun and exposure to re-light the frozen scene and pick a style preset, then export the frame as a PNG re-rendered from the atlas at 4x, as an SVG of text cells, or as plain text.
- **Radial shockwave refraction** (small, practical): Let shock rings also push the glyph lookup outward for a moment through the existing shimmer texture, made radial, so a blast visibly bends the screen around it.
- **Retro bitmap font packs per game** (small, practical): Let `game.json` choose the atlas font: JetBrains Mono, IBM Plex Mono, VT323, Departure Mono, or openly licensed bitmap recreations of IBM VGA and Amiga Topaz baked without anti-aliasing at integer scale. In a text engine, the font is the biggest single style lever.
- **Rich-text markup for HUD strings** (small, practical): `hudText` accepts inline tags such as `{amber}`, `{wave}`, `{shake}`, `{blink}` and `{type:30}` (typewriter at 30 characters a second with chip blips). Dialogue and alerts get personality without custom drawing code.
- **Scorch, soot and track decals** (small, practical): A per-cell stain byte darkens the albedo where blasts scorched and exhaust sooted the rock, and footpads, wheels and dragged chunks leave tracks in loose ground; stains fade with wind or rain. The terrain keeps a record of what happened on it. *Blasts already carve terrain and heat its rims; this keeps a visible mark after the heat fades.*
- **Specular highlights from normals and lights** (small, practical): A material `shine` term (Blinn-Phong from the cell normal and the direction of the sun or a lamp) brightens faces turned toward a light. Highlights then slide over metal and ice as lights move, which goes beyond today's random `*` glints on sunlit shiny cells. *Regolith's He-3 crystals and pad metal would catch the lander's lamps.*
- **Speech bubbles anchored and lit in-world** (small, practical): `bubble(entity, text)` draws a word-wrapped `.--.` frame with a `\` or `/` tail that follows the speaker, keeps clear of other bubbles and the screen edge, and types itself out. An option draws it into the world instead of the HUD, so lamplight and shadow fall on it and dust clouds can hide it.
- **Sprite windows that reflect the sky** (small, practical): Glass cells in a sprite sample the parallax sky layer, mirrored, so the lander's window shows a sliver of Earth that moves as the lander turns.
- **Trauma-based camera shake styles** (small, practical): Replace the single `FX.shake` value with trauma, squared into shake and driving smooth noise, and offer styles: cell-snapped retro jolts, sub-pixel rumble, directional recoil along the blast impulse, and roll once the camera can rotate. Big blasts feel heavier while small thuds stay subtle.
- **Velocity-aligned streak glyphs** (small, practical): Fast sparks, debris and bodies leave one to three trailing cells whose glyph follows the velocity angle (`-`, `/`, `|`, `\`) and fades along the trail. This is motion blur that stays inside the character set.
- **World-anchored material glyph patterns** (small, practical): A material can declare a small glyph tile anchored to world coordinates, such as brick `[__]` over `_][_`, plating `|=|` or mesh `#.#.`, which the light ramp still brightens and darkens. Walls read as masonry or metal, and blast holes cut the pattern cleanly.
- **X-ray silhouette when the player is hidden** (small, practical): When the player sprite is behind foreground terrain or a layer, draw its outline in a dim colour through the rock so the player is never lost.
- **Colour-grading LUTs blended by area** (medium, practical): A 3D LUT (16 or 32 cubed) applied after tone mapping, in the shader and in JS for 2D, and blended by camera position: cold blue caves, a warm surface, a sickly radiation zone. The editor can import `.cube` files from any grading tool.
- **CRT post-process stack** (medium, practical): Optional shader passes for barrel curvature, scanlines across glyph pixels, an aperture grille or shadow mask, rounded bezel corners and a slight bloom bleed, each one a tunable. The 2D fallback does only scanlines and vignette.
- **Immediate-mode ASCII UI widget kit** (medium, practical): `ui.button`, `ui.slider` drawn as `[====|----]`, `ui.check` as `[x]`, plus lists, tabs and text fields with a blinking `_`, all focusable by keyboard, gamepad and touch and with chip sounds. Every game gets settings and pause menus without hand-placed `hudText`.
- **Multi-layer parallax with depth haze** (medium, practical): Replace the single sky layer with `defineLayer(depth, painter)`: far ridgelines generated from the level's own noise at low frequency, mid ruins, and near foreground grass or pipes. Each layer scrolls by its depth, fades toward a haze colour and uses thinner glyphs the farther away it is, and foreground layers fade out when the player is behind them.
- **Per-cell sub-pixel offset channel** (medium, practical): Add a per-cell offset texture (dx, dy in pixels) that the shader applies to the glyph lookup. Sprites and chunks then glide smoothly between cells instead of hopping, while the terrain stays on its grid.
- **Picture-in-picture and minimap windows** (medium, practical): Render a second world view into a HUD rectangle with its own camera and scale: a rear camera, a close-up of the landing pad, or a minimap of the explored world downscaled through the sprite glyph matcher with a blinking `@` for the player. *The pixel-art-to-glyph matcher gets a second job.*
- **Scattering sky gradient in dithered glyphs** (medium, practical): Compute the sky colour per row from the sun's elevation, deep blue overhead and orange glow at the horizon on the sun's side. Shade it with density glyphs ` .:-=` instead of flat fills so the sky still reads as text.
- **Signed-distance-field glyph atlas** (medium, practical): Bake the atlas as an SDF or MSDF instead of coverage bitmaps, so glyphs stay crisp at any scale and outlines, soft halos, drop shadows and emboss all come from one texture lookup. It is the groundwork for smooth zoom, neon and per-glyph lighting.
- **Sky painter preset library** (medium, practical): Ready-made sky painters with their own tunables: nebula clouds, ringed planets, banded gas giants, a galaxy band, a curved planet horizon and a sun disc that follows `setSun`. A new game picks its sky in one line.
- **Camera shake from low-frequency energy** (tiny, bold): Derive screen shake from the actual sub-bass energy of the mix instead of a value set per event, so every boom, collapse and kick drum shakes as hard as it sounds.
- **Comic onomatopoeia from blasts** (tiny, bold): Big blasts can spawn a slanted big-font KRAKOOM or THUD in world space that punches in, shakes and fades, sized by the blast's energy.
- **Film grain in the dark** (tiny, bold): In very dark cells, nudge the ramp index slightly each frame like sensor noise, so night scenes feel filmic and dark gradients stop banding. Its strength follows exposure and the reduced-motion setting.
- **Lightning-flash silhouette frames** (tiny, bold): For a frame or two during a strike, draw the world as black silhouettes against a white-blue sky, so shapes hidden in the dark briefly show. *The engine already tones flashes down for CALM players, and this would follow the same setting.*
- **Neon sign text as emissive glyphs** (tiny, bold): `neonText(x, y, 'OPEN', colour)` writes emissive letter cells that light the world in their own shapes and flicker like failing tubes. When shot, they go dark and fall. *In a text engine, a sign is just a string.*
- **Sleeping bodies puff little z glyphs** (tiny, bold): When the rest timer puts a body or chunk to sleep, it lets out a small drifting 'z' particle. It's a joke, and it also shows at a glance which objects the engine has stopped simulating.
- **Squash and stretch by glyph substitution** (tiny, bold): On landing or impact a sprite briefly swaps glyphs for wider or flatter ones (`o` to `O` to `_`, `|` to `:`) and then springs back. Parts such as antennae get secondary motion from a tiny damped spring.
- **Wind made visible as drifting streaks** (tiny, bold): An optional layer of faint `~`, `-` and `'` streaks that follow the wind field's streamlines through open air. It sets a mood and also helps debug the wind.
- **Big blasts knock HUD letters loose** (small, bold): A blast near the edge of the screen jolts the nearby HUD glyphs. They swing on springs and snap back into place, and a really big one leaves a single letter dangling until the next keypress.
- **Damage glitch effect** (small, bold): On a hit or an EMP, for a few frames shift random glyph rows sideways, swap cells for random glyphs, split the R, G and B atlas samples and drop the chip audio into bit-crushed noise. Games trigger it through one shared `FX.glitch` amount.
- **Depth of field by glyph softening** (small, bold): Out-of-focus layers swap their glyphs for lower-coverage ones and average colours with their neighbours, a blur that stays pure ASCII. Cutscenes can rack focus between layers.
- **Diegetic HUD on the objects themselves** (small, bold): Draw HUD elements into the world, lit and hidden like everything else: a fuel bar on the lander's flank, numbers on the pads, a warning blinking on the hull.
- **Font weight as a second ramp axis** (small, bold): Bake regular, bold and light weights into the atlas and let brightness pick the weight as well as the glyph. Hot or lit cells get heavier strokes and gas and haze get hairlines, which doubles the tonal range with the same characters.
- **Fractional fill levels drawn as glyphs** (small, bold): Store a fill amount for each liquid or powder cell and draw part-filled cells as `_ . , - ~ =` by level. A puddle's surface then reads smoothly at RES 1 instead of stepping in whole cells. *Glyphs sit at different heights inside the cell, which gives sub-cell resolution for free.*
- **House-style assemble animation for panels** (small, bold): Port the hornbaek.org ascii-assemble look into the engine: a bright line traces the frame with sparks at its heads, the title opens out from the centre and the rows print in through flickering noise, then it all comes apart again on close. `msgBox` and menus build themselves instead of popping up. *The games would share one visual language with the user's own site and its existing skill.*
- **Ink-on-paper inverted render mode** (small, bold): Flip the tone map so light is paper and dark is ink: a warm off-white page with glyph colours subtracted from it and emissives in red ink. The same games then read like a printed broadsheet.
- **Lit ASCII portraits with chip lip-sync** (small, bold): Large dialogue portraits are made from pixel art with sprite2ascii and lit by the scene's real lights. The mouth glyph (`o`, `-`, `O`) switches with each spoken syllable.
- **Pen-and-ink cross-hatching shadow mode** (small, bold): An alternative shading path that hatches cells by how deep they sit in shadow (`/`, then `X`, then `#`) in one ink colour on paper white, like an engraving. It is a whole new look from the same exact sun shadows. *The hatching follows real light because Glyphite already computes exact hard shadows.*
- **Phosphor persistence trails** (small, bold): Keep last frame's colour texture and blend it in with a per-channel decay (P31 green lingers, red fades fast), so sparks, rockets, lasers and lightning leave glowing smears like a vector monitor. It is off under reduced motion.
- **Shadow-play silhouette style preset** (small, bold): A Limbo-like preset: a bright backlit sky gradient, every solid drawn as a flat dark silhouette, and only emissive glyphs and particles in colour.
- **Sky painter driven by the music meter** (small, bold): Pass the chip synth's meter to the sky painter so a game can make an aurora or starfield sway with the bass lane and flash on snare hits.
- **Thermal and night-vision render modes** (small, bold): Vision modes that show the heat field as an iron-bow false-colour map over a grey world, or amplify low light into noisy green. They work as goggles in gameplay and as a debug view of heat. *The engine already simulates heat but has no way to view it.*
- **Tilt parallax on phones** (small, bold): Device orientation shifts the parallax layers and the HUD by a few pixels, so tilting the phone gives the world depth. It asks for permission and respects reduced motion.
- **ASCII video player, benchmarked on Bad Apple** (medium, bold): A `.gvid` format stores glyph-plus-colour frames as run-length deltas, encoded by a Python tool that reuses the sprite best-match code. It plays cutscenes and intros in sync with the chip synth, with 'Bad Apple!!' as the benchmark.
- **Back wall that catches offset shadows** (medium, bold): A non-colliding back-wall grid, generated with the world and darkened by ambient occlusion, drawn wherever cells below the skyline are empty. Lamps light it and foreground cells cast shadows onto it offset along the light direction, which stops stars showing in caves and gives them 2.5D depth.
- **Custom post shader hook, hot-reloaded** (medium, bold): `GAME.post` supplies a GLSL snippet, with an optional JS fallback for 2D, that runs on the final colour, and the web editor recompiles it live. Effects like a scanner sweep or a drunken wobble need no engine change.
- **Glyph morph cross-fades between frames** (medium, bold): When a cell changes glyph, the shader blends the old atlas glyph into the new one over about 80 ms, or steps through glyphs of decreasing coverage (`#`, `%`, `+`, `.`). Flowing sand and flickering lamps then look fluid instead of jittery.
- **Glyph shaders that choose the character** (medium, bold): A per-cell GLSL function glyphFn(x, y, t) that returns a glyph index and colour, compiled into the atlas shader, so plasma, tunnels and rotozoomers run fully on the GPU as text. The 2D fallback runs the same function written in JS. *Gives demosceners a classic text-mode effect pipeline, which a post shader cannot do because it only recolours pixels.*
- **HDR output on HDR screens** (medium, bold): Where the browser supports it, render to an extended-range display-p3 canvas so explosions, the sun and glowing lava go brighter than paper white on HDR phones and monitors. The current tone map stays as the SDR path.
- **Image and video backdrops as glyphs** (medium, bold): `defineBackdrop(png or webm)` converts a picture or video into glyphs on the sky layer each frame using the sprite matcher, for a photographic Earth, painted horizons or ASCII cutscenes.
- **Monochrome glyph-density lighting mode** (medium, bold): Terrain tops already pick ramp glyphs by luminance. This mode drops colour entirely and lets light choose the glyph of every cell, slopes, sprites and particles included, from sparse `.` and `:` to dense `#` and `@`: a monochrome terminal look that still shows the lighting.
- **Overstrike: two glyphs in one cell** (medium, bold): Line-printer art: print two printable characters on top of each other ('/' over '\\', 'O' over '-', '\_' under letters) to get denser shapes and more tones. Pairs are measured into the atlas like single glyphs, and the output stays pure ASCII. *Classic line-printer technique, and it stays inside the 95-character rule*
- **Palette-limited glyph-and-colour solver** (medium, bold): With a small fixed palette, choose glyph and palette colour together so that coverage times colour best matches the target brightness (a dim colour becomes a bright colour on a thinner glyph), and diffuse the error to the neighbours. Floyd-Steinberg dithering done with characters.
- **Per-pixel emboss lighting inside glyphs** (medium, bold): Use the SDF gradient as a stroke normal and light each glyph's strokes with the cell's surface normal and the sun direction. A `#` on a sunlit slope then has a bright edge and a dark side, so the text looks carved out of the rock.
- **Sub-cell shadow edges with slash glyphs** (medium, bold): Where a shadow boundary crosses a cell diagonally, draw `/`, `\`, `_` or `'` in the lit side's colour instead of making the whole cell lit or dark. This anti-aliases by choosing a glyph whose shape matches the coverage. *Only an ASCII engine can anti-alias with glyph shapes, and the exact hard shadows say where each edge runs.*
- **Subpixel LCD glyphs for triple horizontal detail** (medium, bold): Render glyph coverage into the R, G and B subpixel stripes separately, ClearType-style, so thin edges and sub-cell positions read at three times the horizontal resolution on ordinary LCD screens. It would be an opt-in WebGL path with a 2D canvas fallback that ignores it. *Squeezes more detail out of the fixed 95-glyph alphabet without adding a single new character.*
- **Water reflections with a glyph-flip table** (medium, bold): Liquid and flat ice surfaces mirror the rows above them, dimmed and tinted, with a per-row ripple that blasts disturb, and each glyph is flipped through a table (`/` and `\`, `^` and `v`, `'` and `,`, `M` and `W`). Lamps and Earth shimmer in a lake.
- **Rotated world with upright glyphs** (large, bold): A camera angle where each screen cell samples the world at a rotated position but keeps its glyph upright. The world can then tilt for roll shake, banked turns and sideways-gravity levels while text stays readable.
- **Smooth camera zoom with ASCII mipmaps** (large, bold): Continuous zoom: zooming in scales the SDF atlas, and zooming out picks each screen cell's glyph from the coverage and average colour of the NxN world block beneath it, cross-faded between levels. That makes zoom punches, overview maps and framing for every player possible. *RES adds characters but never zooms, so zoom is the missing camera axis.*
- **Particle glyphs swell with loudness** (tiny, crazy): Particle and spark glyphs climb the density ramp (`.` to `:` to `*` to `#`) as the master bus gets louder, so a loud moment visibly thickens the air.
- **Velocity shear: italic speed** (tiny, crazy): Shear the glyphs of fast-moving sprites into an italic slant along their direction of travel, a typographic speed line.
- **Faint lit-air tint behind glyphs** (small, crazy): An opt-in mode in which the dark space behind glyphs takes a very faint tint from the light field, so a lamp-lit cave glows between the characters. It breaks the one-colour-per-cell rule on purpose, which is why it is a mode worth trying rather than a default.
- **Ligature fusion: arrows that fuse** (small, crazy): When neighbouring cells form -&gt;, =&gt;, != or ==, the renderer draws JetBrains Mono's ligature across both cells. That can become a puzzle where you arrange glyph debris into symbols.
- **Typewriter imperfection shader** (small, crazy): Add per-cell baseline jitter, uneven ink density, slight rotation and the odd overstrike ghost, seeded by world cell so they stay put, over a faint paper grain. It pairs with paper mode and a clacky chip sound for HUD text.
- **VHS tape mode** (small, crazy): Tracking-noise bands crawl up the screen, colour bleeds to the right, horizontal sync wobbles and a `PLAY >` counter sits in the HUD corner. It is a found-footage skin for horror games or replays.
- **Your handwriting as the glyph font** (small, crazy): Scan the 95 printable ASCII characters handwritten on a template sheet and turn them into a glyph atlas, so a game is drawn in its author's own handwriting. It is still ASCII, only in a different font.
- **ZX Spectrum attribute clash mode** (small, crazy): Quantise colour to one ink and one brightness per 8x8-cell block, as the Spectrum did, so the lander bleeds its colour into the rocks it passes. It is a joke mode that is also a striking art style.
- **Per-cell glyph rotation and scale** (medium, crazy): A second channel holds an angle and a scale for each cell so the shader can rotate and stretch glyphs: tumbling debris letters, spinning `|` propellers, wobbling jelly and sprites that squash on landing.
- **Physics transitions: dissolve to sand or shatter** (medium, crazy): Between scenes, turn the current frame's glyphs into real particles that pour away as sand, or into rigid chunks that crack open and tumble off, showing the next scene behind them. *The transition runs on Glyphite's own sand automaton and chunk system.*
- **WebXR hologram of the world on your desk** (medium, crazy): In WebXR AR on a phone, the GL glyph renderer draws onto a floating plane anchored to a table. Sky, terrain and particles sit on separate depth layers, so walking around the plane gives real parallax.
- **A slower speed of light: relativistic ASCII** (large, crazy): Set c to about 30 m/s and the renderer draws each cell as it was when its light left, with Doppler colour shift and aberration around a fast lander.
- **First-person raycaster view of the grid** (large, crazy): A Wolfenstein-style camera mode treats the voxel grid as a top-down map and casts one ray per screen column, choosing glyphs from distance and the existing normals and lighting. A wall blasted open in the side view becomes an open corridor in the first-person view. *It reuses the same destructible world data and only adds a second way of projecting it.*
- **Oscilloscope vector mode** (large, crazy): Draw each glyph as single-stroke vector paths (a Hershey-style font covering ASCII 32-126) and output them as stereo XY audio, so a real oscilloscope can display the game. In the browser, a phosphor-persistence shader imitates the scope. *The chip synth's audio pipeline can carry the XY signal.*
- **Raymarched 3D SDF scenes drawn in ASCII** (moonshot, crazy): A fragment-shader mode raymarches signed-distance 3D scenes and picks each cell's glyph from coverage and normal direction (`/ \ | -` on edges). It gives fully 3D ASCII scenes such as a spinning moon or a tunnel flight, with Glyphite's bloom and per-cell colour. *The WebGL glyph-atlas shader already picks a glyph per cell, so it only needs a raymarch in place of the voxel grid.*

## Light, atmosphere & weather

- **Light flicker and pulse presets** (tiny, practical): Any light can take `flicker: 'candle' | 'fluorescent' | 'strobe' | 'dying' | 'alarm'`, deterministic from a seed. A dying tube buzzes on the chip noise channel in step with its flicker.
- **Light visibility queries for gameplay** (tiny, practical): `isLitBy(light, x, y)` and `sunAt(x, y)`, alongside the existing `lightAtCell`, tell a game which light reaches a point and whether the sun does. Plants can grow toward light, solar panels charge, vampires burn and stealth games know when you are seen.
- **Stars twinkle through air, fade near lamps** (tiny, practical): Stars scintillate only when the game declares an atmosphere, never on the Moon. Stars near bright lamps dim by an amount of light pollution measured from the light buffer.
- **Sun colour from airmass or kelvin** (tiny, practical): Compute the sun's colour per channel as exp(-scatter x airmass(elevation)), or from a blackbody table through `setSun({kelvin})`. A low sun then turns orange and red by itself, so sunsets look right without a hand-tuned palette.
- **Thunder delayed by distance** (tiny, practical): After a flash, the chip noise channel plays thunder delayed by distance divided by 343 m/s and muffled with range, so players count the seconds to judge how close the storm is.
- **Auto-exposure eye adaptation** (small, practical): Measure the average scene brightness each frame from a small mip of the bloom bright-pass and ease the tone-map exposure toward it. Flying out of a dark cave into sunlight dazzles for a moment before settling, and a cave is black at first and then slowly becomes readable.
- **Beat-reactive emissive materials and bloom** (small, practical): A material property such as `beatGlow: 0.5` and a bloom-pulse hook let glowing rock, crystals and haze pulse with the kick drum. Regolith's beat-synced beacons become an engine feature.
- **Directional sprite shading from auto normals** (small, practical): Derive a normal for each sprite cell from the pixel art's alpha edges, or from a painted height channel, and shade sprites by the direction of the sun and each lamp instead of only the light level at their cell. A lamp on the left then lights the lander's left flank.
- **Drifting clouds with dappled cloud shadows** (small, practical): Clouds of `(`, `~` and `)` drift across the parallax sky. Their coverage, projected along the sun direction, becomes a 1D mask on the sun term, so patches of shadow slide across the land.
- **Global wind with noisy gusts** (small, practical): `setWind(vx, vy, gust)` pushes particles, smoke, light bodies and liquid surfaces with a noise-driven gust profile. The chip synth's noise channel plays a wind sound that follows its strength.
- **Glyph lens flares and glare crosses** (small, practical): Draw the sun disc on the sky layer at the `setSun` angle with a glare halo, and give very bright lights a cross of `-` and `|` cells that fade outward. When the sun is on screen, a chain of `o`, `O` and `( )` ghosts runs through the screen centre on the HUD layer.
- **Lamp beams revealed by kicked-up dust** (small, practical): Particles inside a cone light's lit area brighten and scatter it, so headlights and lamp posts show beams when exhaust stirs up dust. In clean air the beams stay invisible. *Regolith's exhaust already scours sand into dust particles.*
- **Lamp lifecycles: warm-up, pop and afterglow** (small, practical): Sodium lamps start dim pink and warm to orange over a few seconds, and they flicker when their mount shakes. When a lamp breaks it pops with a last flash and its filament glows down over half a second.
- **Moon phases, moonlight and earthshine sync** (small, practical): Draw sky bodies with their phase computed from the sun direction, and let a lit moon act as a faint blue night sun that casts shadows. Regolith's earthshine ambient would follow Earth's phase.
- **Sun from any angle by sheared sweep** (small, practical): Replace the left-to-right column sweep with a sheared sweep along any direction, so the sun can stand overhead, shine from the right, or sit low under a cliff. Below the horizon it fades out by itself. *It goes beyond the roadmap's mirrored sweep for the right side and covers every angle with one code path.*
- **Day-night cycle driver** (medium, practical): `setDayCycle({length, path, palettes})` moves the sun along an arc and blends sun colour, ambient, sky gradient and star density from dawn through dusk to night, turning emissive lamps on at dusk. A game gets time of day from one call, with no code per frame. *It builds on `setSun`, `setAmbient` and the existing sky layer.*
- **Rain with velocity glyphs, splashes and puddles** (medium, practical): Raindrops whose glyph follows their velocity (`|`, `/`, `\`, `'`) and the wind burst into `.` and `,` splashes on impact, darken and gloss the surfaces they wet, and collect into puddles as water cells.
- **Snow that piles, drifts, avalanches and melts** (medium, practical): Snowflakes settle as a light granular material that piles more steeply than sand, drifts with the wind into lee slopes and packs into ice under weight. A blast or a heavy landing shakes a slope loose as an avalanche, and snow melts near lamps, fire and hot exhaust.
- **Soft sun penumbra knob** (medium, practical): Give the sun an angular size: the sweep traces 3 to 5 rays per column at slightly different angles and averages them, so shadow edges blur in proportion to the caster's distance. Size 0 keeps Regolith's razor-sharp vacuum look.
- **Valley fog that pools and drifts** (medium, practical): A low-resolution fog density field settles into dips below the skyline, drifts with the wind and burns off in direct sun. It draws as faint `~` and `-` glyphs lit by the sun and lamps.
- **Weather presets with blended transitions** (medium, practical): `setWeather('storm', {ramp: 20})` blends cloud cover, wind, precipitation, fog, sun dimming and the odds of lightning from the current state over time. Each game defines its own presets.
- **City lights on Earth's night side** (tiny, bold): Regolith's Earth shows faint orange `.` and `:` clusters along its continents on the night side, turning slowly with the planet. *The sky painter already draws continents from fbm noise and a lit and unlit side.*
- **Hailstorms that bounce and chip** (tiny, bold): Hail particles bounce off terrain with restitution, chip soft materials, rattle on the chip noise channel and melt into puddles.
- **Acid rain and volcanic ash fall** (small, bold): Weather that changes the world: acid drops eat soft materials and leave pitted `:` surfaces, and volcanic ash settles as a grey granular layer that dims the sun and buries the terrain.
- **Aurora curtains on the sky layer** (small, bold): Wavy vertical curtains of `|`, `!` and `:` in colours shifting from green to magenta, animated by noise and folding slowly. They cast a faint coloured ambient onto the snow below.
- **Bioluminescent fungi pulse when disturbed** (small, bold): Fungus cells flare when particles, bodies or the player touch them, and the glow ripples outward from cell to cell like a wave before fading.
- **Dune migration by wind** (small, bold): A slow wind field moves surface granular cells downwind over minutes, burying wrecks and uncovering crystals, with dust streaming off the crests.
- **Eclipses with corona and sudden night** (small, bold): A moon on the sky layer crosses the sun disc. Sun intensity follows the uncovered area, ambient drops, stars come out, and a ring of `*` glyphs flares around the disc as a corona.
- **Field of view from the shadow caster** (small, bold): Reuse point-light shadow casting from the player's eye as roguelike field of view: cells the eye cannot reach are hidden, and cells seen earlier stay remembered in dim blue-grey. Stealth games and fog of war come almost for free. *Light comes first in Glyphite, so sight and lighting are one system and a torch really does let you see further.*
- **Fireflies as boids of tiny lights** (small, bold): Swarms of `.` particles flock as boids, prefer dark air, scatter away from the player and each splat a tiny light, so a meadow at dusk glows and moves.
- **Fog of war remembered by light** (small, bold): Any cell lit above a threshold gets a 'seen' bit: unseen caves stay black and seen but unlit cells draw dim. Exploring with a lamp maps the cave as you go.
- **Frost that lingers in shadow shapes** (small, bold): Cells open to the sky collect a white `'` frost overlay at night. At sunrise it sublimates only where the sun hits, so for a while the frost keeps the exact outline of every shadow. *Shows off the engine's exact hard shadows.*
- **Gobo patterns for cone lights** (small, bold): A cone light can carry a 1D angular mask, such as window bars, a fan's turning blades, a lighthouse beam or a film projector, so its light and soak project a pattern across the walls.
- **Heiligenschein: bright halo round your own shadow** (small, bold): Ground cells close to the anti-sun point, where your own shadow falls, get brighter. This is a real regolith effect (opposition surge), so on the Moon the lander's shadow wears a glowing halo. *A real lunar optics effect that the exact sun-shadow solver can place*
- **Meteor showers that sometimes land** (small, bold): Night-sky streaks of `-` and `*` with short trails. Rarely, one enters the world layer and calls `explode()` where it hits, leaving a glowing hot crater.
- **Phosphorescent glow-in-the-dark materials** (small, bold): A material property `phosphor: {charge, decay}` stores the light a cell receives and re-emits it slowly. Sweeping a flashlight across a cave leaves glowing breadcrumb trails.
- **Photoreactive materials and shadow puzzles** (small, bold): Materials react to how much light they get: ice melts in sun, a mould dies in light, a crystal charges in it. Puzzles are solved by moving objects until their hard shadows fall on the right cells.
- **Rainbow arc at 42 degrees** (small, bold): When it rains on the side away from the sun, paint a ROYGBIV arc of `(` glyphs on the sky layer at 42 degrees around the anti-solar point. It fades when the rain stops.
- **Real lunar day and Earth phase** (small, bold): The Worker or the page computes today's lunar phase from the date, and from it Earth's phase as seen from the Moon and the sun angle of the lunar day. Regolith's sky then matches the real one: at full moon players see a new Earth overhead.
- **Real star catalogue night sky** (small, bold): Build the star layer from the Bright Star Catalogue, with magnitude picking `.`, `+` or `*` and colour index picking the tint, and rotate it around the pole with the time of day so real constellations appear.
- **Sunlight that heats, shadow that chills** (small, bold): Use the exact sun shadow pass to warm lit surface cells and cool shaded ones. Ice then survives only in permanently shadowed craters, and a lunar dawn melts the frost line by line. *Reuses Glyphite's exact hard sun shadows as an input to the heat field.*
- **Thermals rising off sunlit ground** (small, bold): Sunlit and hot cells push warm air upward in the wind field, so smoke and dust columns rise over lit ground and lava, and gliding objects can ride the thermals.
- **Translucent materials glow from within** (small, bold): Ice, crystal and gel let light seep a few cells into the solid with per-channel falloff, a cheap subsurface scattering. A lamp behind an ice wall turns its inner cells blue, and crystals glow through thin rock.
- **UV lamps and fluorescent minerals** (small, bold): Add an invisible UV light channel: a UV lamp lights nothing until it hits fluorescent materials, which glow back in their own visible colour. Prospectors could sweep a dark cave to find He-3 veins. *A whole mechanic from one extra light channel in the existing point and cone light code.*
- **Atmosphere presets: Moon, Mars, Earth, Titan** (medium, bold): `setAtmosphere({density, scatter, dust})` derives the sky colour, sunset hue, fog, penumbra, star twinkle and star visibility, and muffles explosions to a thud in vacuum. One line picks a planet's look.
- **Binary suns with coloured shadows** (medium, bold): Allow two or more suns, each with its own sweep and colour. Where only the red sun is blocked the shadow is lit blue by the other, giving Tatooine-style double coloured shadows.
- **Caustics on floors below liquids** (medium, bold): Refract the sun through the slope of each water-surface column and add the bent rays into a 1D buffer at depth, so the floor below a lit pool shows shifting bright `~` and `=` caustic bands.
- **Directional skylight ambient** (medium, bold): Replace the single ambient colour with skylight that depends on how much of the sky each cell sees and in which direction. Overhangs go dark, shadows facing open sky turn bluish, and cells facing a sunset horizon pick up its orange.
- **Dust storms that swallow the sun** (medium, bold): Strong wind lifts surface sand into airborne dust that thickens the fog, reddens and dims the sun and cuts visibility to lamp range. Players navigate by beacon lights until it clears.
- **Lava glow lighting ash plumes from below** (medium, bold): Lava and other very hot materials light the smoke and ash particles above them from below, so volcanic plumes glow orange at the base and fade to grey as they rise and drift with the wind.
- **Lightning by dielectric breakdown** (medium, bold): Bolts grow as a random walk biased toward tall, conductive and wet cells, drawn as `/`, `\` and `|` polylines with bloom and a world-wide flash, and they run along metal materials. The strike heats and blasts its target, lights point lights along the bolt for two frames, and fuses sand into glass fulgurites through the existing `fuseTo`.
- **Line and area lights for tubes** (medium, bold): Lights shaped as segments or rectangles, such as neon tubes, glowing seams or a lit doorway, sample several points along their length. Their shadows come out soft and stretched instead of point-sharp.
- **Mirror and glass materials that bend light** (medium, bold): Material flags `reflect` and `refract` make sun, point and cone light bounce off and bend at the cell normals the lighting pass already computes. A mirror can throw sunlight into a cave, and laser-routing puzzles become possible. *It uses the per-cell normals (LNX/LNY) the lighting pass already has.*
- **Seasons that recolour the world** (medium, bold): A slow season clock shifts material tints (vegetation from green to orange to bare), sun height and day length, turns rain into snow in winter and sheds leaves as particles from tree materials in autumn, so one level plays in four looks.
- **Smoke and dust cast moving shadows** (medium, bold): Splat particle density into a coarse grid that the sun sweep and point-light rays treat as partial transmittance. Explosion smoke then throws drifting, dimming shadows across the terrain.
- **Stained glass: coloured transmittance materials** (medium, bold): A material property `transmit: [r,g,b]` makes light rays multiply their colour through a cell instead of stopping. Glass, crystals and gels then cast coloured light and coloured shadows.
- **Underwater light with depth tint** (medium, bold): Light passing through water loses red first and then green (per-channel Beer-Lambert absorption in the ray march), so the deep turns teal and then black. Floating `.` particulates and wobbling light shafts from the surface complete the look.
- **Volumetric light shafts through dusty air** (medium, bold): Trace the sun and cone-light visibility through air cells that hold dust, fog or smoke, and draw faint `.` and `:`, or `/`, `|` and `\` matched to the light angle, tinted by the scattered light. Shafts through cave mouths and lamp cones appear exactly where the shadows say the light goes. *Reuses Glyphite's exact shadows, so the rays are physically correct.*
- **One-bounce colour bleeding via virtual lights** (large, bold): Cluster sunlit surface cells into coarse virtual point lights tinted by their albedo. A sunlit red cliff then warms the shadowed cave beside it.
- **Dust devils that wander and lift sand** (small, crazy): Small vortices form over hot sunlit flats, wander with the wind, spin grains and light debris up a twisting `(` and `)` column, and collapse when they reach shade or rock.
- **Earth eclipse turns the Moon copper** (small, crazy): In Regolith, Earth slides in front of the sun. Direct light goes out and the surface is lit only by a thin red ring of refracted Earth sunsets, which tints the regolith copper. *Regolith already paints Earth on the sky layer and uses earthshine as its ambient.*
- **Real local time sets the sun** (small, crazy): The Worker injects the visitor's approximate latitude and local time from `request.cf`, so the sun angle and day or night match the view outside their window. *It uses what the Cloudflare edge already knows about each request.*
- **Black-hole material that bends light** (medium, crazy): A gravity-well material pulls particles and bodies with an inverse-square force and bends the sun rays and cone lights that pass near it, drawn with an ASCII accretion ring.
- **Orbiting point sun round a planetoid** (medium, crazy): For small round worlds with gravity as a vector, a sun at a finite distance orbits the planetoid and casts radial shadows across the whole map. Day and night then move around the globe.
- **Polarising filters and polarised light puzzles** (medium, crazy): Light rays carry a polarisation angle; filter materials pass cos-squared of it and rotator crystals turn it. Puzzle rooms open only when the right brightness reaches a sensor through crossed filters.
- **Prisms split white light into rainbows** (large, crazy): Give each colour channel its own index of refraction and bend rays by Snell's law at material boundaries. A white beam entering a glass wedge then fans out into red, green and blue bands on the far wall.
- **Radiance cascades global illumination in WebGL** (moonshot, crazy): Compute full 2D global illumination with radiance cascades on the occupancy texture in fragment shaders: soft shadows, bounce light and emissive materials lighting rooms in real time. Lava glows up a shaft, a lamp lights the far wall of a cave, and crystals bleed colour onto the rock around them. *Radiance cascades suit a 2D grid world especially well.*

## Physics & simulation

- **Bubbles rising through liquids** (tiny, practical): Gas trapped under a liquid rises as wobbling `o O` bubble cells and pops at the surface with a tiny blip on the pulse channel.
- **Conveyor and bounce material properties** (tiny, practical): Add `surfaceVel` and `restitution` per material, so conveyors carry grains and bodies and rubber pads launch the player.
- **Dust clouds that linger after collapses** (tiny, practical): A big chunk landing or a roof caving in raises a slow cloud of dust particles that hangs, drifts and dims the light for a few seconds.
- **Ropes that snap under tension** (tiny, practical): Give each rope a `maxTension`: an overload, a blast or the loss of its anchor cell cuts it at the weakest link, and the two halves whip away.
- **Acid that eats through softer materials** (small, practical): Acid eats the cells it touches faster the softer (lower `tough`) they are, using itself up and giving off fumes. Players can dissolve a path through soft rock but not through steel.
- **Any cell edit triggers the support check** (small, practical): Today the support check runs only after `explode()`; expose `checkSupport(x, y, r)` and call it from digging, acid, melting, burning and player-placed or removed cells. Undermining a cliff with a drill or a fire then brings it down too.
- **Arcs, sparks and short circuits** (small, practical): Water touching a powered cable, or two charged objects drifting close, throws a jittery arc of bright glyphs with spark particles and a crackle on the noise channel.
- **Continuous collision for fast projectiles** (small, practical): Sweep fast bodies and particles along their path through the grid with a DDA, so bullets and meteors never tunnel through thin walls at RES 3.
- **Density layering and displacement** (small, practical): Let a cell swap with a lighter fluid beneath it, by a per-material `density`. Oil floats on water, sand sinks through it, and a falling rock chunk pushes the lake up around it.
- **Embers, charring and blowing ash** (small, practical): Burning wood passes through a dark char stage with flickering `*` embers that pulse in the glow channel, then crumbles into ash grains that a draft can blow away.
- **Explosive materials and chain reactions** (small, practical): Cells with `explosive: power` go off when heated past a threshold, so a fire that reaches a gunpowder vein sets off a chain of blasts along it.
- **Force fields API** (small, practical): `addField({ kind: 'radial' | 'vortex' | 'directional', x, y, r, k })` pushes bodies, particles, grains and liquids, so one call makes a fan, magnet, tractor beam, whirlpool or black hole.
- **Friction and impact heating** (small, practical): Sliding bodies, grinding chunks and fast impacts add heat to the cells they touch, so a skidding lander leaves glowing scorch marks and a meteor arrives white-hot.
- **Gear trains, belts and pulleys** (small, practical): Ratio constraints between rigid bodies, with belts drawn as glyph lines that slip under load. Players can build winches, clocks and lifts that move real chunks.
- **Gravity zones painted as regions** (small, practical): `addGravityZone(rect or mask, gx, gy)` overrides gravity for bodies, particles and grains inside it, so sand falls sideways in one room, flips upward in another, and a VVVVVV-style level is only data. Builds on the roadmap's gravity vector.
- **Mud and quicksand** (small, practical): A thick half-liquid, half-granular material that slows bodies by depth, keeps footprints and slowly swallows anything that stops moving.
- **Penetration depth and ricochet by toughness** (small, practical): Projectiles and drills bore into cells to a depth set by their momentum against each material's `tough`, and glance off at shallow angles with sparks. Bullets punch through dirt and ricochet off steel.
- **Ripples on liquid surfaces** (small, practical): Run a 1D spring height-field along every liquid surface, so landing bodies, blasts and wind make ripples that travel and reflect. The surface is drawn as `_ - ~ ^` by slope.
- **Shaped charges and directional blasts** (small, practical): Give the explosion API a cone direction and a focus so a charge can drive a narrow jet through rock, or throw debris one way only. Demolition games get breaching charges and cutting cords, not just round craters.
- **Sinter regolith into bricks and landing pads** (small, practical): Focused heat from a laser or exhaust fuses loose regolith into a tough sintered material once the heat field passes a threshold. Players can 3D-print pads, walls and ramps in place, much as real lunar ISRU proposes. *Reuses the heat field and blast-rim fusing to turn destruction physics into construction.*
- **Splash droplets that become liquid again** (small, practical): A body or debris entering a liquid throws droplet particles that turn back into liquid cells where they land, the way grains already do. A chip-noise splash scales with impact speed.
- **Transparent glass that shatters into shards** (small, practical): A see-through material that passes tinted light in the sun and point-light passes and breaks into sparkling shards with a high chip tinkle. It gives Regolith's blast glass a use.
- **Buoyancy and drag for bodies and chunks** (medium, practical): Sample hull points against liquid cells to apply lift from displaced volume plus quadratic drag. Crates float, the lander sinks slowly in a methane lake, and a pumice chunk bobs.
- **Burning materials and spreading fire** (medium, practical): Add `flammable`, `ignite`, `burnRate` and `burnTo`, so wood, oil and fuel catch from heat and spread fire cell to cell. Burning cells give off fire particles, smoke and light and leave char and ash.
- **Chunk-to-chunk collision from cell masks** (medium, practical): Test falling chunks against each other by overlapping their rotated cell masks and resolve with the existing impulse contacts, so rubble piles up, boulders knock chunks aside and broken roofs stack instead of passing through each other. Expands the roadmap item.
- **Heat conduction with per-material conductivity** (medium, practical): Spread the existing heat field each step, weighted by each material's `conduct` and `heatCap`. A metal beam carries a fire's heat into the next room, rock insulates, and hot lander legs cool slowly.
- **Kinematic chunks: doors, lifts, moving platforms** (medium, practical): Chunks that follow scripted paths instead of gravity and carry the bodies standing on them (feet inherit the platform velocity). Blasted, they break and fall as normal chunks.
- **Lava that glows and cools into rock** (medium, practical): A hot fluid that holds its heat at 1.6, so the existing heat light makes it glow; it sets flammables alight, melts fusible materials, and hardens into basalt with a crust glyph once it cools below a threshold.
- **Liquid materials in the cell automaton** (medium, practical): Add `flow: 'liquid'` with `viscosity` and `spread` to defineMaterial, so water, oil and molten metal fall like sand, then flow sideways up to N cells a step, level out, fill craters and drain through gaps. It reuses the sand step's awake mask and unlocks lakes, flooding, rain puddles, drowning lemmings and underwater levels. *The granular automaton and its awake tracking already exist, so liquids are the natural next rule.*
- **Material reaction table** (medium, practical): `defineReaction(a, b, { to: [c, d], chance, heat, light, sound })`, checked by the automaton wherever two materials touch: water + lava gives obsidian + steam, acid + metal gives gas, spores turn rock into moss. Acid, rust, ignition, growth and alchemy then need no game-specific automaton code. *A table of rules is easy for a Claude session to write and extend from a one-line request.*
- **Melting, freezing and boiling thresholds** (medium, practical): Add `meltAt/meltTo`, `freezeAt/freezeTo` and `boilAt/boilTo` per material. Ice turns to water beside a lamp, water freezes in shadow, and steel goes molten when a reactor overheats.
- **Slice bodies and chunks along a cut** (medium, practical): Split a rigid chunk's cell mask along a line into two new bodies, sharing out its momentum and spin. Laser cutters, blades and thrown discs can then cut structures the way Fruit Ninja cuts fruit.
- **Verlet ropes and chains** (medium, practical): `addRope(a, b, segments)` makes chains of point masses with distance constraints that collide with the grid and tie to bodies or cells, drawn as `- / | \` by segment angle (`o` for chain links). It covers grappling hooks, winches, tow cables for broken chunks, rope bridges, fishing lines and pendulums.
- **Wheels, suspension and drivable vehicles** (medium, practical): A wheel body with rolling friction from the ground material, a suspension spring and a torque motor, so a moon buggy can drive, pull wheelies and spray regolith grains from spinning tyres.
- **Joints and motors between rigid bodies** (large, practical): Hinge (with limits and a motor), piston, weld, distance and spring-damper joints with break thresholds, in a small sequential-impulse solver. The lander's legs become a general engine feature, and cars, ragdolls, catapults and drawbridges whose parts snap off as chunks become possible.
- **Bioluminescent fungus on damp dark rock** (small, bold): A creeping material spreads over wet, unlit rock and gives off a faint glow, so dark caves slowly light themselves in patches.
- **Cloth and flags shaded with glyphs** (small, bold): A grid of Verlet points shaded by its normal through the material ramp, for flags, banners and sails. The same flag hangs dead still on the airless moon and ripples in wind on Earth.
- **Ditto material that mimics what is above it** (small, bold): A material drawn with the glyph and colour of the cell above it, like a ditto mark, for secret walls. It shows itself as '"' when hit or lit by a UV lamp. *An ASCII pun that works as a mechanic*
- **Earthquakes and seismic waves** (small, bold): A shake wave travels out through solid cells from an epicentre, loosening cells at random by toughness and starting structure collapses far from any blast.
- **Expanding foam that sets into structure** (small, bold): A sprayable material that swells to several times its volume over a few seconds, flows into gaps, then hardens into a breakable solid that joins the support graph. Players can patch hulls, plug sand flows or build bridges mid-collapse.
- **Game of Life moss** (small, bold): A material property such as `life: 'B3/S23'` runs Conway-style rules every N frames: the moss pulses and spreads, blasts seed gliders, and with glow on the live cells light the cave.
- **Glyph-ink hitboxes from atlas coverage** (small, bold): Each glyph's pixel coverage mask from the font atlas decides collisions, so two sprites collide only where their ink overlaps. A bullet can pass through the hole in an `O` but not through a `#`.
- **Granular arching and hourglass flow** (small, bold): Sand jams into a stable arch over a narrow gap until a blast, vibration or a pull from below releases it, and flow through an opening runs at a rate set by its width, so hourglass timers and hopper puzzles work.
- **Heavy gases pooling in hollows** (small, bold): Gases denser than air (CO2, poison, cold fog) flow downhill and pool in pits as faint `~` glyphs that dim the light, giving caves deadly hollows the player can see and avoid.
- **Methane pockets that ignite as flame fronts** (small, bold): Methane cells hidden in rock leak out when exposed, and a spark sends a flame front racing through the gas cloud. A natural hazard for Cave Drone.
- **Oobleck: solid when struck, liquid at rest** (small, bold): A non-Newtonian material that acts as solid wherever local impact speed or shear passes a threshold and flows like liquid otherwise. You can run across it but sink if you stand still, and blasts bounce off it.
- **Rust and slow corrosion** (small, bold): Metal touching water slowly turns into weaker orange rust cells, so old structures sag and finally collapse through the normal support check.
- **Sand compacts into sandstone under load** (small, bold): Granular cells buried under enough weight for long enough turn into a weak solid that holds overhangs and can break as structure. Old dunes become cliffs, and blasting them returns loose sand.
- **Weight-sensitive cracking floors** (small, bold): Body contacts add load to the cells they rest on, and brittle materials show it as `'` then `,` then `x` before breaking out as chunks. Heavy enemies, or an overloaded player, fall through rotten floors.
- **Welding chunks back into structures** (small, bold): A heat tool or hot contact fuses touching chunks into one body, or bonds a resting chunk back onto an anchored structure, using the fusing that already happens at blast rims. Players can repair or build with debris.
- **Ant colony with a pheromone field** (medium, bold): A pheromone field next to the heat field lets hundreds of single-glyph ants dig tunnels through the falling sand, carry grains home and lay trails, building an ASCII ant farm as you watch.
- **Bendable metal that dents and buckles** (medium, bold): Materials that are not brittle deform on impact: cells shift along the hit normal and lose toughness, so steel hulls dent and roofs buckle instead of chipping.
- **Bodies plough and sink into sand** (medium, bold): Two-way coupling between rigid bodies and granular cells: a heavy body pushes grains aside into berms, sinks by its pressure, and leaves tracks and skid trenches. A lander touching down too hard digs itself a crater.
- **Bodies that shatter into their glyph cells** (medium, bold): A sprite-based rigid body hit past its strength breaks along its sprite cells into tumbling chunks that chip and freeze like structure pieces. This gives the backlog's lander damage model (one leg snaps, the craft tips) a general mechanism.
- **Clinging electrostatic lunar dust** (medium, bold): Sunlit regolith dust picks up charge, hovers in a thin layer at the terminator and sticks to lamps, visors and solar panels, dimming them until wiped. This is a real Apollo problem turned into a mechanic. *Light and dust are both first-class in Glyphite, and Regolith Lander already throws dust everywhere*
- **Communicating vessels and water pressure** (medium, bold): Track each connected body of liquid and its surface height, so U-tubes level out, siphons work and a hole in a dam wall squirts water at a speed set by its depth.
- **Electric power through conductive cells** (medium, bold): Materials get a `conductive` flag, and each tick power flood-fills from sources through connected conductive cells. Cables feed lamps, doors and motors that go dead when a blast cuts the wire.
- **Fuel slosh shifts the lander's balance** (medium, bold): Each fuel tank holds a tiny liquid sub-grid inside the rigid body, so the centre of mass moves as the craft tilts and brakes. Late in a burn a half-empty tank sloshes and tips the lander unless the pilot counter-steers. *Reuses the cell automaton inside a body; the fuel level is visible as glyphs in a cutaway HUD tank*
- **Hot-air balloons lifted by the heat field** (medium, bold): An envelope of cloth cells traps hot gas from the air grid, and buoyancy comes from the temperature difference. A burner, or a fire below, inflates it, and holes let the heat leak out.
- **Hydraulic erosion and sediment** (medium, bold): Flowing water picks up granular cells as sediment and drops them where it slows, carving gullies and building deltas while you watch.
- **Ice that refracts, melts and shatters** (medium, bold): A translucent ice material that slightly bends and tints light, melts into water near heat or in sun, and breaks along structure lines into glinting shards. Frozen lakes become something to play with.
- **Magnetic materials and electromagnet cranes** (medium, bold): Materials with a `magnetic` strength respond to field sources, so an electromagnet crane can lift loose iron chunks and a magnet tool can pull ore grains out of sand.
- **Plants that grow toward the light** (medium, bold): A `grow` behaviour for vine, moss and seed materials that extends L-system stems and leaves (`| / \ Y & %`) into empty cells where `lightAtCell` and nearby water allow, leaning to the brightest neighbour and withering in darkness. Moving a lamp makes a vine bridge grow across a gap. *The engine's ray-traced light field becomes food for plants.*
- **Pressure soft bodies and slimes** (medium, bold): A blob is a ring of masses on springs plus internal gas pressure, so slimes squash, jiggle and squeeze through gaps, filled with glyphs by their area.
- **Steam boilers and pressure vessels** (medium, bold): Heating a sealed tank of water builds steam pressure that drives a piston, blows a whistle on the pulse channel, or bursts the tank past its rating.
- **Voronoi asteroid fields of loose rock** (medium, bold): Voronoi-fractured, unanchored rock bodies drift in zero gravity, and blasts split them along their cell borders into smaller tumbling chunks.
- **Voronoi fracture patterns per material** (medium, bold): Precompute Voronoi cells per material, so structures split along shard shapes (slabs for concrete, splinters for wood, chips for glass) instead of round blobs.
- **Wet sand turns cohesive mud** (medium, bold): A per-cell moisture value from rain or water darkens sand, raises its angle of repose and lets it hold steep walls and overhangs or clump into chunks, drying out in sunlit cells first. Sandcastles stand until the sun dries them and they slump, so a storm visibly changes how the terrain behaves.
- **Wing lift and drag for gliders** (medium, bold): Compute lift and drag from a body's cell outline and its angle of attack, scaled by the atmosphere preset's air density. Gliders, flapping wreckage and parachutes then behave differently on Mars, Titan and Earth, and not at all on the Moon.
- **Coarse air grid for smoke and gas** (large, bold): A Jos Stam stable-fluids solver at a quarter of cell resolution, with solid cells as walls, carrying pressure, smoke, steam and poison. Gas fills rooms and leaks out of holes, smoke curls around rocks, rotors push air, blasts leave real swirls, and particles and bodies feel the flow as wind (optionally drawn as `> ^ < v`).
- **Load paths and stress in structures** (large, bold): Estimate how load flows through a structure at each support check, so overloaded beams sag (their glyphs bend) and snap where stress peaks, not only where they lose their connection.
- **FLIP particle-grid liquids for real splashes** (moonshot, bold): A hybrid particle-grid (FLIP/PIC) liquid solver that throws and sloshes water with real momentum, then writes its particles back into cells for drawing and lighting.
- **Anti-sand that falls up and piles on ceilings** (small, crazy): Gravity gets a per-material sign in the granular automaton, so these grains rise, form upside-down dunes and pile up under overhangs. Mixed with ordinary sand it makes puzzles that work like an hourglass in reverse.
- **Brazil-nut sorting when sand is shaken** (small, crazy): Give granular materials a grain size, so shaking from an earthquake or a vibrating plate brings big grains and buried chunks to the surface. Hidden crystals rise out of the regolith.
- **Joke materials: bubble wrap and popcorn** (small, crazy): Bubble wrap pops (o to .) with a noise click when weight presses on it. Popcorn kernels heated by the heat field jump as particles and land as fluffy '&'. Both double as test materials for the reaction table.
- **Radioactive decay material** (small, crazy): A material with a half-life that randomly transmutes into a daughter material, releasing heat (so it glows) and clicks on the noise channel like a Geiger counter. A buried ore vein warms the rock around it.
- **Thermal shock and frost weathering** (small, crazy): Sudden temperature changes (lava meeting water, a hot hull quenched) crack brittle cells into rubble, and water freezing in cracks slowly splits rock apart.
- **Wireworld logic circuits in the world grid** (small, crazy): A material whose cells follow the Wireworld rules (electron head, tail, conductor), so levels or players can build working gates and clocks out of ASCII and then blast them apart.
- **Comet ice that outgasses jets in sunlight** (medium, crazy): A volatile ice material that sublimates when the sun sweep lights it and vents gas particles that push nearby bodies and chunks. Sunlit comet surfaces then hop, spin and crumble as the day passes. *Combines exact sun shadows, the heat field and exhaust particles into one emergent effect.*
- **Explosive decompression of sealed rooms** (medium, crazy): When a blast breaches a pressurised moon-base room, air rushes out through the hole for a few seconds, dragging loose sand, particles, crates and the player toward the breach. *Regolith Lander's base and roof already break, and the setting is a vacuum.*
- **Time-dilation bubbles** (medium, crazy): Regions where bodies, particles, sand and heat step at a fraction of the normal rate, so a slow-motion field freezes a falling roof mid-collapse and the player walks under it.
- **Vibrations travel through connected structures** (medium, crazy): Spread impact energy along the support graph used for breakage, so hitting a girder rings at the far lamp post and tapping a cave wall reaches anything resting on the same rock.
- **Draw falling-sand rules as before/after patterns** (large, crazy): Define new automaton behaviour by drawing 3x3 ASCII before and after patterns with probabilities, as in the Powder Toy or Sandspiel rule editors, and the engine compiles them into the sand step.
- **Light-fed ecosystem with grazers and decay** (large, crazy): Moss grows only in lit cells, grazer particles eat it and breed, predators hunt the grazers, and fungus turns corpses back into soil. Shadows from structures you build or blast reshape the food web, and the player farms by placing lamps. *Exact sun shadows and light soak become a resource.*
- **Logic circuits from wires, plates and gates** (large, crazy): A wire material carries a spark that spreads one cell per step (redstone or Powder Toy style), with pressure-plate, switch, gate, piston and lamp materials. Puzzles and traps are built in the grid, and blasting a wire really cuts it.
- **Neural cellular automaton material that regrows** (large, crazy): A material whose update rule is a tiny trained neural cellular automaton (as in Growing NCA, a few KB of weights) run in a WebGL pass, so a coral reef or alien hive regrows its pattern after you blast it.
- **Oxygen, smothering and backdrafts** (large, crazy): A coarse oxygen field that fire uses up: sealed rooms smother their flames, opening a door feeds a backdraft fireball, and vacuum levels cannot burn at all.
- **Point-mass gravity for tiny planetoids** (large, crazy): Gravity summed from point masses, so sand slides around a round voxel asteroid, characters walk all the way around it, and blast debris goes into orbit before raining back down. It takes the roadmap's gravity vector all the way to Mario Galaxy-style platforming and orbital-mechanics lessons.
- **Portals that carry bodies, grains and light** (large, crazy): Linked portal pairs that pass rigid bodies, particles, falling sand and light rays through with rotated momentum. Sand pours endlessly through a loop, and a lamp shines out of the far side.
- **Glyph-shaped collision** (moonshot, crazy): An optional mode where a terrain cell's collision shape is the actual ink of its glyph, so a ball rolls along the curve of `(` and slips through the gap in `:`. What you see is exactly what you hit.

## World & generation

- **Geodes and lava tubes** (tiny, practical): Generator features for hollow spheres lined with glowing crystal material, and long smooth tubes with skylights where the roof has thinned.
- **Idle world ticks: a pebble falls while you wait** (tiny, practical): After about 20 seconds with no input, the engine wakes one tiny event: a grain trickles from an overhang, a dust mote drifts through a lamp beam, or a chunk settles with a creak.
- **Maze generators with ASCII-native walls** (tiny, practical): Recursive backtracker and Wilson's algorithm write mast-style walls, with corridor width in base units so the maze scales with RES.
- **Poisson-disk scatter for pickups and props** (tiny, practical): scatter(rng, {minDist, mask, maxSlope}) places crystals, lamps and boulders on valid surfaces using colTop and normalAt. It replaces the placement loops each game writes by hand.
- **Probabilistic glyphs in map legends** (tiny, practical): Legend entries like '?': {rock: .5}, '\*': ore-or-rock and '\~': loose sand let a hand-drawn map play slightly differently on each seed while keeping its authored shape.
- **Seed-stable sub-seeds per feature** (tiny, practical): Each generation feature gets its own RNG from hash(seed, 'caves'), hash(seed, 'crystals') and so on, so changing the crystal count in the editor doesn't move the caves. *Editor tunables change one thing precisely instead of rerolling the whole world.*
- **Erosion pass at generation time** (small, practical): Thermal (talus) and hydraulic erosion run on the height profile before rasterising, then a few simGranular iterations. The result has gullies and scree slopes that look sculpted rather than noisy.
- **Generator pipeline of named, toggleable passes** (small, practical): game.json lists the generation passes in order (strata, caves, erosion, craters, scatter), each with its own parameters and sub-seed. The editor can switch a pass off or step through them to see what each one did, and every pass parameter can be a tunable.
- **Levels as plain ASCII text files** (small, practical): games/&lt;id&gt;/levels/\*.txt where each character is the material's own glyph ('#' rock, '.' sand, '\*' crystal, 'P' pad, 'L' lamp), mapped through a legend in game.json or a header line. Each character becomes a RES-scaled block, so one map works at every detail level, and the code editor or a painter can read and write it. *A text-only engine gets levels that look like the game itself, the format Claude writes most easily and a pull request diffs line by line.*
- **Noise toolkit: ridged, Worley, domain warp, curl** (small, practical): Add ridged2, worley2 (distance and cell id), warp2 (domain warping) and curl2 next to fbm2. All are seeded and take scales in base units so they stay RES-aware.
- **Prefabs and stamps drawn in ASCII** (small, practical): definePrefab('hut', rows, legend, {anchors}) is placed with stamp(p, x, y, {flip, rot90}), and named anchors become spawn points for mounts, lights and pickups. In the editor you can drag a box over part of the world (a lamp post with its mount, a ruined roof) and save it as a prefab text file.
- **Reachability check for generated levels** (small, practical): After generation, flood-fill the free space using the player's own collision box and jump or thrust reach, to prove the pad, every pickup and the exit can be reached. If one can't, reroll or carve a passage.
- **Seed explorer for procedural terrain** (small, practical): A grid of headlessly rendered thumbnails of the worlds for seeds 1-64. Click one to play it, star favourites, and write a curated seed list into game.json.
- **Strata, faults and ore veins** (small, practical): A layered geology pass: material bands by depth, a fault line that offsets them, folds from a sine warp and ore veins that follow one stratum. Cliff faces become readable glyph texture.
- **Trigger zones and spawns from level files** (small, practical): A second key layer in level files (magenta spawn points, cyan trigger zones and yellow checkpoints in a PNG, extra legend characters in a text map) feeds a general zone registry with onEnter(zone) and onExit hooks.
- **Biome registry with Whittaker selection and blending** (medium, practical): defineBiome({fill, surface, ambient, sky, music, particles}), with the biome per column (or per region, top-down) placed by hand or chosen from temperature and moisture noise so ice, dust and lava belts emerge. Borders blend materials with dithered noise, and sky, ambient light and music crossfade as the camera passes.
- **Infinite vertical dig shaft** (medium, practical): Rows are generated on demand below the camera, with strata, caves and ores that change with depth (Motherload or Downwell style). It is a cheap first step toward full streaming.
- **Linked sub-worlds behind doors** (medium, practical): A game holds several grids (a surface, a mine, a building interior), and doors or shafts switch between them. Each keeps its own destruction, light and sky, so a world can be bigger than one grid without streaming.
- **PNG import with noise-edged per-RES upscale** (medium, practical): Extends the roadmap's colour-key PNG import to upscale per RES with noise-dithered edges and automatic slope glyphs. An optional greyscale PNG can set a heat or glow mask.
- **Seeded cave and dungeon generator library** (medium, practical): Gen.caCaves (the 4-5 smoothing rule), Gen.worm tunnels that follow curl noise, Gen.drunkard, and Gen.bsp rooms joined by L-shaped corridors, all seeded and writing materials straight into the grid. Doors can be a weak material that blasts open, the calls work side-view or top-down at gravity 0, and the roguelike, metroidvania and colony kits would all share them.
- **Shared breakable prefab library in engine** (medium, practical): A catalogue in engine/prefabs/ of tested set pieces (antenna mast, rope bridge, scaffold, bunker, stone arch) tuned to collapse well under the support check. Each has a preview on the portal.
- **Spelunky-style room templates with guaranteed route** (medium, practical): Small ASCII room templates tagged by their exits (LR, LRD, LRU) are chained along a random walk through a 4x4 room grid. Every level has a solution path and still feels hand-made.
- **Precarious arches generator** (tiny, bold): A pass that deliberately leaves arches and overhangs that only just pass the support check, so one well-placed blast brings down a whole ceiling.
- **Sand spouts and hourglass caves** (tiny, bold): A level feature pours granular material from a ceiling cell forever (or for N cells). It makes sand rivers the player must route, dam or blast.
- **Self-healing terrain that regrows from the seed** (tiny, bold): Destroyed cells slowly regrow toward the generator's original state, with rock creeping back in from the edges of a crater. Arena and multiplayer maps never get dug flat, and the heal rate becomes a tunable.
- **Craters made by simulating real impacts** (small, bold): Build crater fields by firing N virtual meteors through the engine's own blast code during generation (carving, ejecta, fused rims). The starting terrain then matches what blasts do later in play. *The physics engine generates its own levels.*
- **Hidden gas pockets and lava voids** (small, bold): Sealed pockets of pressurised gas or lava are placed underground and vent or explode when the player digs into them. They add risk and reward beneath the surface.
- **L-system trees and crystal clusters** (small, bold): L-system grammars grow into mast-style materials anchored at the root, so a tree falls as one chunk when its base is cut.
- **Lore carved into the terrain** (small, bold): An LLM writes inscriptions from a lore bible in the game folder, and they are stamped into rock as a glyph material, lit and shadowed like everything else. Blasting the wall destroys the lore, and sand slowly buries it. *Text is literally terrain in Glyphite.*
- **Real lunar terrain from NASA elevation data** (small, bold): A build-time tool slices LRO LOLA elevation data into 2D cross-sections (Shackleton's rim, Tycho, the Apollo 11 site), bakes them into compact arrays and adds regolith depth and boulders. You can land where Apollo did.
- **Secrets that exist only at 3x detail** (small, bold): Small cosmetic features that only fit at RES 3: an ant trail in a crack, a name carved on a lamp post, tiny footprints. Physics is unaffected, so the world still behaves the same at every detail level. *Gives players a reason to try the detail levels*
- **Text and ASCII art as destructible terrain** (small, bold): stampText('GAME OVER', font, ROCK) builds banner-font letters out of materials, and any ASCII art file (classic archives, figlet output, a friend's drawing) imports as terrain, with material and colour chosen by each character's ink density. The title screen becomes rock you blast apart letter by letter into tumbling chunks. *An ASCII engine whose words are physical objects.*
- **Tides and rising flood levels** (small, bold): A global water level rises and falls on a timer (a tide, a flood, a sinking ship), and a liquid material fills caves through whatever openings there are. It first needs a flowing liquid in the material registry.
- **Cutaway interiors behind blasted walls** (medium, bold): Buildings generate room interiors (furniture glyphs, desk lamps) on the back-wall layer, revealed only when the facade is blasted away.
- **Difficulty-targeted generation with rerolls** (medium, bold): The generator measures each level (cave width, pad slope, distance, fuel needed) and rerolls sub-seeds until it falls inside a difficulty band. That makes 'difficulty' an honest tunable.
- **Procedural buildings designed to collapse** (medium, bold): Towers with storeys, girder masts, floor decks and anchored foundations, plus a bracing parameter. A game can tune how a building comes down in pieces when blasted. *Breakable structures already exist; this gives them architecture worth breaking.*
- **RES-independent levels from SDF shapes** (medium, bold): A level is a list of shapes in metres (circle, capsule, polygon, noise-edged blob) with materials, rasterised at any RES by stampSDF. Hand-made levels then look and play the same from 1x to 3x.
- **Stitch authored set pieces into noise** (medium, bold): Hand-made set pieces are placed along a generated world, with the seams blended by signed-distance falloff. Authored moments then sit naturally inside procedural terrain.
- **Turn a photo into a level** (medium, bold): Upload a photo: edge detection and k-means colour clustering map its regions to materials (sky transparent, dark rock, bright ice, mid-tones sand), then Claude names the materials and chooses their properties. A photo of a mountain range becomes a playable landing site.
- **Volcano with a pressurised magma chamber** (medium, bold): A buried chamber builds pressure until it erupts ejecta, lava flows and an ash cloud. The ash darkens the sky layer and rains down as grey grains.
- **Wave function collapse from ASCII samples** (medium, bold): Draw a 30x12 sample in a text file. Overlapping-model WFC learns its 3x3 neighbourhoods and grows a level of any size in the same style, with entrances and exits pinned as constraints so the rooms still connect. *The sample and the output are both plain text, which is the engine's native medium.*
- **Wrap-around worlds for planetoids and Asteroids** (medium, bold): worldWrap: 'x' | 'xy' makes getC, the sun sweep, the camera, bodies, particles and light wrap at the edges. A small moon can be flown all the way round and its craters seen again from the other side, and Asteroids-style arenas (rocks as rigid chunks that split where shot) just work.
- **City skyline generator with lit windows** (large, bold): Blocks of mixed-material buildings with emissive window cells and point lights that go dark floor by floor as the structure collapses. It is a demolition game in a box.
- **Daily level made by Claude** (large, bold): A scheduled session generates a new level, briefing and music variation each day, and the autopilot checks it can be beaten before deploy. Players compete on a shared leaderboard.
- **Geology time-lapse world generation** (large, bold): Generate a level by fast-forwarding its history at build time: lay down rock strata, fold and fault them, then run meteor impacts, sand settling, wind erosion and lava flows for thousands of steps. The terrain gets believable layers, ejecta blankets and caves along the bedding planes.
- **Level briefs with constraints that are checked** (large, bold): Ask for something like 'narrow chimney, lava lake on the left, three crystal pockets, pad reachable on 60% fuel'. A generator and the search autopilot loop until every constraint holds, then report what they verified.
- **Lock-and-key metroidvania world graph** (large, bold): A graph grammar generates a mission graph (key to door, ability to gate) that is then laid out as rooms and corridors. A validator confirms every key is reachable before its lock.
- **Prompt-to-level through a terrain DSL** (large, bold): An LLM writes a level as a short program in a constrained DSL (fill, carve, noise, cave, place structure, light) that the engine runs deterministically. 'A buried temple over a lava lake' becomes a playable map, saved as the DSL text.
- **Streamed infinite worlds with saved diffs** (large, bold): Generate the world in fixed chunks from (seed, chunk x, chunk y) as the camera moves and unload chunks that are far away. Only the cells the player changed are kept, as compact diffs per chunk, so an endless map stays destructible and remembers every crater.
- **Structure architect that maximises satisfying collapses** (large, bold): A headless search places breakable structures and scores them by the collapse chains they produce in simulation when hit. Levels ship with towers that topple like dominoes.
- **Drop any file to see its bytes as rock** (small, crazy): Drag a file into the game and its bytes become strata, with each byte value picking a material and glyph. A JPEG, a zip and a PDF make visibly different geology.
- **Any web page's layout becomes terrain** (medium, crazy): Paste a URL; a Worker renders it with Browser Rendering, reads each element's box, and turns the boxes into breakable structures labelled with the page's own text. You can then blast your company homepage apart. *Text-native layouts map directly onto a text-native world, and the Worker already sits on Cloudflare.*
- **Levels generated from a MIDI file** (medium, crazy): Drop in a .mid and the world is built from it: pitch becomes terrain height, drums become craters and sustained notes become bridges. The song plays as you fly through it.
- **Ruins made by actually demolishing buildings** (medium, crazy): Generate an intact base, run a headless simulation with random blasts and support checks for a few hundred steps, then freeze the result. The ruins really collapsed instead of being drawn.
- **Your street from OpenStreetMap as a level** (medium, crazy): A tool that fetches building footprints and heights for a lat/long and extrudes them into a side-on breakable skyline, or a top-down double-wide level, with prefab interiors. Missile Command or rescue-chopper kits could then be played over your own town.
- **Polar world: a whole asteroid you orbit** (large, crazy): Draw the grid in polar coordinates around a centre and point gravity toward it, so a level is a complete tiny planet you can circle (as in Mario Galaxy). Sun shadows sweep round as it turns.
- **Terrain that only decides when lit** (large, crazy): Unlit regions stay undecided. When light first reaches a cell, the generator settles its contents to fit its neighbours, so throwing flares carves the world into existence. *Uses the ray-traced light as the trigger for generation*
- **Slice-switching: a 3D voxel world seen in 2D** (moonshot, crazy): Store the world as a 3D voxel volume and draw one 2D slice of it, which the player shifts or rotates (as in Fez or Miegakure), so a wall in one slice is a tunnel in the next. Sand and blasts are simulated in all three dimensions.

## Entities & gameplay systems

- **Ballistic arc preview** (tiny, practical): Draw the predicted path of a throw or shot as fading `.` glyphs, computed with the same integrator and gravity as the real projectile. The arc stops at the first solid cell.
- **Birds that perch, then scatter at blasts** (tiny, practical): Glyph birds (`v` and `^`) land on mounts and roof edges and burst away on blast or collapse events. It is a cheap bit of life that makes the world react.
- **Explosive barrels that chain through heat** (tiny, practical): A barrel body detonates when the heat under it passes a threshold or it takes a hard impact, so one blast cooks off the next. Chain reactions and fuel depots come straight from the existing heat and blast code.
- **Hit feedback kit in one call** (tiny, practical): `hitFx(ent, dir, n)` does a 40 ms hit-stop via `FX.slow`, flashes the sprite's glyphs white, applies a knockback impulse and pops a damage number with `addText`. Every game gets crunchy combat for free.
- **Throwable flares and glowsticks that settle** (tiny, practical): Small rigid bodies that carry a point light, bounce realistically, get buried by falling sand and slowly dim. Players can mark a route through a dark cave and come back to find it.
- **Tiny state machine helper with labels** (tiny, practical): `fsm({idle:{enter, update, to:{chase: e => e.sees}}})` with a `stateT` timer per entity. The current state name is shown above the entity in the debug overlay and reported by `probe()`.
- **Boids swarms: bats and fireflies** (small, practical): A `defineParticle` flavour with separation, alignment and cohesion through the spatial hash, plus wall avoidance via `normalAt`. Bats flap between `v` and `^` and scatter from flashes, and emissive fireflies avoid bright cells and gather in dark corners, so caves glow until you switch on a lamp.
- **Bullet-pattern emitters for shmups** (small, practical): `pattern.spiral / ring / aimed / wave` emitters fire cheap particle bullets with a glyph per type (`* o + .`), with graze detection. Patterns are seeded and repeatable, for shmups and boss fights.
- **Climbable, one-way and swimmable material flags** (small, practical): The material registry gains `oneWay`, `climbable`, `liquid` (buoyancy, drag) and `hazard` (damage per second) flags that characters and bodies read. Ladders, jump-through ledges, water and spikes then become plain materials.
- **Day-night cycle that changes behaviour** (small, practical): `setSun` elevation sweeps over a tunable day length, and one `timeOfDay` value in `probe()` drives behaviour. Nocturnal entities wake at dusk, daytime ones shelter in shade, and shops close.
- **Energy accounting overlay for physics lessons** (small, practical): A HUD panel that tracks kinetic, potential, heat and destroyed-bond energy per body and globally, with ASCII bars and a 'where did it go' breakdown after each blast. It makes conservation (and its losses) visible to a class.
- **Factions with a relationship matrix** (small, practical): Faction tags plus a hostility matrix let enemies fight each other, and light-dwellers and dark-dwellers clash at the shadow line. The player can lure one faction into another.
- **Gravity gun: grab, carry and throw** (small, practical): A spring-damper tether pulls any loose body, or cuts a block out of terrain and makes it a body, then throws it with the momentum the player gave it. It turns every breakable structure into ammunition.
- **Health component with damage types and teams** (small, practical): A shared `damage(ent, n, type)` with per-type armour, invulnerability frames, knockback and team filtering, fed by the simulation itself: hot cells burn, `chunkVsBody` impact speed crushes, blast falloff hurts and landing speed deals fall damage. A collapsing roof becomes a weapon, and every kit wires up damage the same way. *Every source of harm already exists in the simulation; this just routes it into health.*
- **Light sensors and colour-keyed doors** (small, practical): A sensor material fires a game event when the light falling on it passes a threshold, optionally for a given colour mix. Doors, lifts and traps can then be wired to light.
- **Material gun: spray any registered material** (small, practical): A Noita and Worms style tool that fires grains of any material from the registry. Sand fills pits, ice makes bridges that exhaust sublimates, and anchor cement stops a structure collapsing.
- **Minecart rails through carved tunnels** (small, practical): A rail material that carts follow with real momentum. A blast breaks a rail and the cart derails into a tumbling chunk, which suits mining and escape sequences.
- **Mining with crack-stage glyphs** (small, practical): A pick or drill chips a cell over a time set by its toughness, showing the damage as `. : * #` overlays. The cell then drops grains that a vacuum collects into material counts.

- **Projectiles with direction glyphs and swept hits** (small, practical): `defineProjectile({speed, gravity, drag, glyphs:'-\\|/', onHit})` sweeps each step against cells, chunks (`chunkAt`) and entity hulls and picks its glyph from the flight angle. It calls `blastFrom` before any explosion.
- **Quest and objective tracker in probe()** (small, practical): `quest('crystals', {goal: 5, text})` adds a HUD checklist and toasts on progress, and `probe()` reports the full quest state. Tests and AI sessions can then assert that a level can be finished.

- **Status effects wired to materials and light** (small, practical): `addStatus(ent, 'burning', 3)` has tick, apply and expire hooks plus built-in looks: burning spawns `PT_FIRE` and a flickering light, frozen tints the sprite cyan and swaps its glyphs to `#`, and wet doubles shock damage. Materials can apply statuses on contact.
- **Trees that fall, burn and rot** (small, practical): Trees are wood structures on the existing support check. Cut or blast the trunk and the crown falls as a chunk that can catch fire, then slowly rots into soil.
- **Weighted spawn and loot tables on seeded RNG** (small, practical): Data-driven tables for pickups, hazards, creatures and `defineItem` loot per biome and depth, rolled on the seeded RNG, give one place to tune what appears where. Rarity shows as the item glyph's colour, and legendary items are emissive, so they light the cave around them.
- **ASCII grid inventory with shaped items** (medium, practical): A HUD panel framed in `+-|` holds Tetris-shaped items drawn as their glyphs, dragged with the mouse or moved with the keys. The weight you carry changes the player body's mass.
- **Branching dialogue in plain-text scripts** (medium, practical): An Ink- or Yarn-like `dialogue.txt` per game, with choices, flags and conditions, linted at build time. It is editable in the web editor with a click-through preview pane, and speakers get ASCII sprite portraits.
- **Cutscene timeline with letterbox and skip** (medium, practical): `cutscene([camTo(x, y, 2), say('...'), wait(1), explode(...), music('boss')])` runs as a coroutine with `=` letterbox rows on the HUD. Enter skips it, and it steps deterministically in tests.
- **Enterable vehicles with spring suspension** (medium, practical): A rover or buggy uses the lander's spring-damper legs as wheel suspension, and the player gets in and out with one key, which hands over input and camera. - **Platformer character controller kit** (medium, practical): `addCharacter({w, h, jump, run})` gives a capsule body coyote time, jump buffering, variable jump height, wall slides and step-up over one-cell bumps against the voxel grid, and reports the ground material for footstep sounds. Platformers and shooters stop hand-rolling movement. *Both current games fly, so a walking controller unlocks a whole genre on the same physics.*
- **Save/load as grid diffs over a seed** (medium, practical): A save regenerates the level from its seed, then applies a run-length-encoded diff of changed cells plus entity, chunk and quest state. Saves of a heavily destroyed world stay a few kilobytes in IndexedDB.

- **Enemy waves that breed from your killers** (medium, bold): Each wave's genome (speed, size, aggression, preferred approach) is bred from the enemies that did the most damage in the last wave. A generation number and a mutation readout on the HUD let you watch them adapt to you.
- **IK legs that plant on voxel surfaces** (medium, bold): Procedural walkers (spiders, mechs) cast each foot to the ground with `normalAt`, step when a leg stretches too far and draw their legs as glyph lines. When a blast takes the ground from under a foot, they stumble. *Extends the lander's spring-damper legs into a general walker.*
- **Laser beam that reflects and melts rock** (medium, bold): `addBeam(x, y, dir, colour, power)` traces a hitscan beam through the grid. It pumps heat into the cell it ends on until that cell melts or vaporises, so holding the beam cuts slots through rock.
- **Moon pet that evolves from its food** (medium, bold): A small critter's genome drifts with the materials you feed it: crystals make it glow and regolith makes it heavy. Its sprite parts mutate over sessions, saved in KV.
- **Pipes, pumps and valves** (medium, bold): Pipe cells move liquid and gas between tanks at a set flow rate and pressure, with powered pumps and valves that can be blasted open. Base-building and factory games get plumbing.
- **Quests built from facts in the world** (medium, bold): A quest generator queries the actual world (crystal clusters, standing structures, stranded bodies) to build fetch, rescue and demolition quests with real coordinates, and shows them in a quest log on the HUD grid. It asks an LLM only for names and flavour text.
- **Shadow creatures that fear and eat light** (medium, bold): Enemies that move only through cells below a light threshold, with their flow field weighted by darkness. They smoke and recoil in lamplight and try to knock your lamps over, and a rarer kind drains nearby point lights and switches off emissive cells it touches, so the level slowly goes dark. *Breakable lamp posts already exist as mounts, so defending lights is a fight over real objects.*
- **Time rewind built from grid deltas** (medium, bold): A ring buffer of changed cells and body states lets the player rewind 10 seconds, as in Braid: sand un-falls and blasts un-carve. The same buffer powers a scrubber in the editor for debugging.
- **In-game contraption builder** (large, bold): Players or level designers weld beams, wheels, thrusters and ropes into machines, Bad Piggies style, simulated by a new joint solver on the rigid bodies. When the machines crash they break into real chunks.
- **Armour as a voxel shell** (medium, crazy): An armoured entity carries a small local grid of shell cells around its sprite, and blasts and bullets chip it cell by cell. You can see the dents and aim for the bare spot.
- **Crafting by simulation: furnaces that really melt** (medium, crazy): There is no recipe menu: dump sand into a furnace structure and heat it with the heat field until it fuses into glass cells, then mine the glass. Recipes are material transitions the world actually runs. *Fusing at blast rims already exists; this extends it into a crafting loop.*
- **In-world terminals that edit the live game** (medium, crazy): Terminals placed in a level show a real tunable or a short hook function. The player types a new value or line and it is swapped in without restarting the level, so a puzzle is solved by reprogramming gravity or a door.
- **Bosses built as breakable voxel structures** (large, crazy): A boss is a moving anchored structure made of grid cells. Shots chip away armour cells, loose limbs break off as chunks, and destroying the anchor weak point drops the whole thing into rubble. *Turns the existing support check and chunk system into a boss fight with no special code.*

The best genomes are stored in KV and become the next level's wildlife. *Real contacts and real granular sand give evolution something honest to learn on.*

## AI-first authoring

- **Asset provenance and licence check** (tiny, practical): Every source image in ref/ and every MIDI in music/ carries a sidecar file with its origin, author and licence (or 'generated by Claude in session X'). The build warns about any asset without one.
- **Edit hook that rebuilds the touched game** (tiny, practical): A Claude Code PostToolUse hook that runs glyphite build &lt;id&gt; after any edit under games/&lt;id&gt; and returns syntax or name-clash errors at once, instead of at the end of the turn.
- **Engine errors written for Claude sessions** (tiny, practical): Every engine error names the API.md section and the usual fix ('defineMaterial: id 300 exceeds MAT\_MAX 256, see API.md#materials'). `Glyphite.run` also checks hook names with did-you-mean ('updat is not a hook') and validates the touch layout and detail settings.
- **Engine requests filed by game sessions** (tiny, practical): When a game session hits a missing engine feature, it files a structured issue labelled engine: the need, the workaround used (file and line), a proposed general API and the other games that would use it. A later engine session batches these into general features and removes the workarounds. *Keeps the engine-vs-game rule intact even when a session is in a hurry.*
- **Glyph shape table for choosing characters** (tiny, practical): Add a table to API.md giving each of the 95 glyphs' ink density, edge direction and centre of mass. A model can then choose glyphs by shape when drawing by hand, without seeing the font.
- **Handoff note for resuming sessions** (tiny, practical): Each session ends by writing STATE.md automatically: what it was doing, what is verified and what comes next. A plain 'Resume' then picks up exactly where it stopped.
- **Prompt log per game, linked to PRs** (tiny, practical): Each request is recorded word for word in games/&lt;id&gt;/PROMPTS.md with its commit and a screenshot, and the editor lists every prompt it sent with its status and resulting PR. You can see how a game was made, which requests landed, and re-send the ones that did not.
- **SPEC.md as each game's contract** (tiny, practical): A short plain-language spec per game (pitch, rules, feel goals, non-goals) that every session reads first and keeps current. The playtest agent grades each build against it.
- **A design critique skill** (small, practical): A `glyphite-critique` skill has bots play the game and screenshot key moments (the first 5 s, the first death, the win). It writes a structured review of readability, contrast, the first 30 seconds and juice, with patches ranked by effort.
- **A skill for adding a new enemy** (small, practical): A `glyphite-new-enemy` skill turns a one-line description into a sprite (through the pixel-art pipeline), a behaviour and stats. It also adds a regression test showing the enemy can reach and hurt the player.
- **API.md that cannot drift** (small, practical): Generate API.md's reference tables from JSDoc and banner comments, and fail the build when an engine global is undocumented or a documented one is gone. Every code block marked runnable is executed against the starter in a headless page, so the examples Claude copies are known to work. *API.md is the first thing every Claude session reads, so it must never drift.*
- **ASCII sprite literals written in code** (small, practical): Entities can define their sprite, parts and animation frames as template-literal ASCII art, a block of glyphs aligned with a block of colour keys plus a legend, alongside the pixel-art pipeline. Claude can then draw and adjust small props and sprites directly in a diff, the way it writes a level map.
- **Before-and-after replay clips in every PR** (small, practical): CI plays the same seeded replay on main and on the branch for each changed game and posts side-by-side animated WebP clips plus a text-frame diff. The reviewer sees what Claude's change does without checking out the branch.
- **Generate a compact context pack** (small, practical): `glyphite context <id>` writes one short brief: the API signatures the game uses, its globals and tunables, the latest HISTORY entries and open ROADMAP items. It is sized for the editor's Claude call and for subagents.
- **glyphite new --kit with genre skills** (small, practical): The CLI scaffolds a game from any kit template, and each kit comes with a Claude skill holding a design checklist: the tunables that matter, a list of juice to add, and known pitfalls. A session can then ship a good game in one pass. *Games are made by Claude sessions, so a kit's skill matters as much as its code.*
- **Morning digest of overnight sessions** (small, practical): When autonomous sessions finish, a Worker builds one page with what changed in each game, before/after clips, test status, cost, and merge or reject buttons. The owner reviews a whole night's work over coffee.
- **One-glance verification contact sheet** (small, practical): `tools/contact.py <id>` renders one labelled image covering 2 renderers x 3 detail levels x 3 key moments (title, mid-run, after a blast). Claude and the reviewer check every combination in a single look instead of a dozen screenshots.
- **Plain wishes become tunable changes instantly** (small, practical): A small, fast model in the Worker maps 'heavier, floatier lander' to tunable changes in under a second. It passes the request to a full code session only when no tunable fits.
- **Pre-baked dialogue written at authoring time** (small, practical): The session that builds a game also writes hundreds of NPC lines tagged by situation, mood and persona into the game folder. ## Runtime AI & NPCs

- **Companion drone that holds your light** (small, practical): A follower on a leash rides the player's flow field and carries a cone light pointed where you aim. You can send it ahead to scout, ping crystals and comment in bubbles, which turns Cave Drone's sunless cave into a lighting puzzle for two.
- **Context barks with priorities and cooldowns** (small, practical): Short NPC lines are picked from tagged tables when events fire (blast nearby, ally down, low health, player spotted), with priorities, per-line cooldowns and one speaker at a time, and shown in speech bubbles. NPCs feel alive with no LLM, and the tables are easy for Claude to write.
- **Lore bible with edge retrieval** (small, practical): Each game's lore.md is embedded into Cloudflare Vectorize at build time, and NPC prompts fetch the relevant facts, so a dozen characters stay consistent about the same moon colony.
- **Markov babble model inlined at build** (small, practical): The build trains a character-level n-gram model on the game's lore and inlines a few kilobytes of tables. NPCs can mutter endless nonsense in the lore's flavour with no API at all.
- **Perception memory with last-known positions** (small, practical): Each agent keeps what it last saw and heard, with a timestamp and a confidence that decays. It searches the last-known cell, then gives up and returns to patrol, and a `?` glyph over its head plus a ghost marker show what it believes.
- **Prompt-injection guard for NPC chat** (small, practical): Player text is fenced as data, NPC outputs are validated (printable ASCII only, length limits, verbs from the action registry only), and a red-team test suite of jailbreak lines runs in CI.
- **Steering behaviours for flying enemies** (small, practical): Seek, arrive, flee, wander, orbit and wall avoidance (feelers checked against the grid with `normalAt`) are composable functions that return a thrust vector. Flying drones, wasps and rival landers get smooth motion without pathfinding.
- **Tiny neural net runtime inlined in page** (small, practical): Glyphite.nn is an MLP forward pass of about 150 lines over Float32Array weights stored as base64 in the page, so policies trained offline run with no dependencies. *It keeps the rule of one page with no runtime dependencies.*
- **Action registry: LLM picks verbs, engine executes** (medium, practical): `defineAction(name, schema, fn)` declares what NPCs can do (walk\_to, dig, say, follow, throw). The LLM may only return JSON tool calls from that list, and deterministic code validates and runs them, so a model can never inject code or break the physics. *It follows the engine's registry pattern (materials, particles, mounts).*
- **GOAP planner for multi-step NPC goals** (medium, practical): A goal-oriented action planner that chains registered actions with preconditions and effects (find charge, place charge, retreat, detonate) and replans when terrain changes. It fills the gap between behaviour trees and LLM brains.
- **Incremental flow fields on destructible terrain** (medium, practical): `flowField(goal, passable)` runs Dijkstra over the cell grid and recomputes only the dirty rectangles after `explode()` or `setC()`, so hundreds of enemies follow one gradient and pour through any hole you blast. A debug overlay draws it as `<^>v` arrows, and tower defense, RTS, colony and chaser games can share it. *A static navmesh is useless when every wall can be blown away; a grid field stays correct for free.*
- **Utility AI with tunable response curves** (medium, practical): Each action (flee, attack, reload, hide) is scored from inputs like distance, health and light level through curves whose knee points are editor tunables. Designers retune behaviour by dragging sliders, with no code.
- **Grenade AI that solves bank shots** (small, bold): Enemies solve the ballistic arc for the current gravity. With no direct line, they simulate a handful of bounce trajectories off the grid to find a ricochet that lands near the player.
- **Mission control capcom with light-time delay** (small, bold): Regolith Lander gets a Houston voice that reacts to telemetry ('60 seconds', 'contact light', 'coming in hot, 3.2 metres a second') in a HUD ticker and as SAM-style chip speech through a band-pass filter with static bursts. Callouts arrive 1.3 s late, the light time from Earth; rules drive it, with optional rate-limited LLM colour through the Worker. *It fits the reference game's Apollo mood and uses the chip synth for the voice.*
- **NPCs notice when you wreck their world** (small, bold): NPCs keep a snapshot of the cells they care about (their house, the bridge) and compare it against the live grid. A missing bridge becomes a fact in their prompt ('who blew up my bridge?') and sours their mood. *A destructible world gives grudges something real to point at.*
- **Panic contagion in crowds near blasts** (small, bold): Crowd NPCs gain fear from nearby explosions, fire and screams, flee along the inverse flow field and pass the panic to their neighbours. A single grenade can make a whole colony stampede.
- **Stealth by real light and shadow** (small, bold): A `visibility(x, y)` helper combines a line-of-sight ray through the occupancy grid with the lighting buffer, so guards spot the player only in cells above a light threshold, and a Thief-style ASCII light gem on the HUD shows how visible you are. Standing in a sun shadow, switching off your lamp or shooting out a lamp post really hides you. *Glyphite's exact shadows become a gameplay mechanic instead of just looks.*
- **AI director that turns the tunables** (medium, bold): A Left 4 Dead style director reads the run's stress (fuel, damage, time since the last success) and nudges live tunables within their declared min and max. The designer's ranges become the AI's guardrails. *Tunables already have honest min/max and a live flag, so the director needs no new configuration.*
- **Behaviour trees as an indented text DSL** (medium, bold): Write AI as a plain-text tree (`? select`, `-> sequence`, leaves that call game functions), parsed at build time. The editor draws the tree with the running node highlighted live in the preview. *A text tree is easy for Claude to write, diff and review in a patch.*
- **Behaviour-cloned ghost of your own flying** (medium, bold): Record your inputs and states, train a tiny MLP in a Web Worker with plain JS backprop, and race a ghost that flies like you. Ghosts of friends are stored in KV behind Access.
- **Claude describes the frame on demand** (medium, bold): Press ? and the current glyph grid, with material names, goes through the Worker to Claude, which answers with something like 'You are drifting left toward the cliff, the pad is below-right' or explains what just exploded. *The frame is already text, so an LLM can read it cheaply without vision.*
- **Enemies that remember you across sessions** (medium, bold): A Durable Object per player stores your habits (landing side, favourite weapon, hiding spots), and enemies set ambushes to match. A 'they know' line warns you at the start of a run.
- **Enemies that understand destructible cover** (medium, bold): Enemies rate cover by raycasting through cells weighted by toughness and pick spots behind anchors. They break from cover when it is blasted, and sometimes blast the player's cover first.
- **Gossip network: NPCs spread what they saw** (medium, bold): Each NPC holds facts with a confidence value and passes them to neighbours on contact, with distortion, so news that you blew up the reactor reaches the far outpost before you do. HUD barks quote the rumour.
- **NPC brain endpoint on the Worker** (medium, bold): A `/api/npc` route behind Access takes a persona card from game.json and a short state snapshot, then streams back a line from Claude or Workers AI that must fit a 38x5 box and may carry one allowed action (give item, open door). Calls are rate-limited per Access user, repeated answers are cached in KV, and offline the game falls back to canned lines. *The Cloudflare Worker and Access login already exist, so the endpoint is private and needs no new hosting.*
- **NPCs that read the world as text** (medium, bold): An NPC perceives the actual glyph grid in its line of sight, clipped by the engine's shadow raycasts, and sends it to the LLM as a 40x20 block of text. It can then say things like 'there's a crystal under that ledge'. *No vision model is needed because every frame is already text.*
- **Post-crash tip from flight telemetry** (medium, bold): After a crash, Claude sums up the last 3 s of `probe()` data (velocity, angle, throttle) in one line, such as 'You came in at 4 m/s tilted 20 degrees; level out earlier.' A rule-based fallback is used offline.
- **Repair drones that rebuild broken structures** (medium, bold): A structure stores its original cells as a blueprint, and after a blast builder drones fly in to place cells back from the bottom up, re-anchoring roofs and remounting lamps. Players race to destroy a base faster than it heals, or protect the drones fixing their own. *The existing support check tells the drones which cells must go back first.*
- **Search-based bot that proves levels beatable** (medium, bold): A planner beam-searches input sequences through `Glyphite.test.step` until it reaches a level's goal, and CI fails if it cannot. Its winning run is saved as a replay and a ghost, so a change that makes a level impossible is caught before merge. *AI sessions change levels all the time, and this gives them proof that the game can still be won.*
- **Slow mind, fast reflexes: async planner module** (medium, bold): Glyphite.mind splits each NPC into a deterministic 60 fps behaviour layer (steering, avoidance, A\*) and a slow async LLM planner that sets goals every few seconds. LLM replies are logged with their tick, so replays stay deterministic. *Model latency never stalls the physics loop, and seeded replays still work.*
- **Squad flanking with weighted flow fields** (medium, bold): Squad members head for the same goal over flow fields with different costs: one avoids the player's line of fire, one prefers dark cells, one blasts through rock. Roles (suppress, flank, dig) and a shared blackboard holding the player's last-known position bring them in from different sides.
- **Tension director for world events and waves** (medium, bold): `defineEvent({weight, when, run})` is scheduled by a Left 4 Dead style director that tracks stress (recent damage, health, time since the last fight) and fires meteor showers that crater the level mid-play, cave-ins, rising lava and dust storms. Enemy waves spawn only in cells the lighting buffer says are dark and off camera.
- **Tunnelling diggers that path through rock** (medium, bold): A\* where each cell's cost is its material toughness, which avoids flowing sand and re-plans when a blast changes the grid. Burrowing enemies, miners and rescue bots then chew along the path and turn cells into `PT_GRAIN`, while anchor materials stay impassable so level designers can fence them in. *Reads the material registry's toughness and feeds the sand automaton directly, so it works in any game.*
- **Colony job queue: dig, haul, build** (large, bold): Players mark cells to dig or build, and workers claim jobs, path there, mine with toughness-based timing, carry grains to stockpiles and place them, as in Dwarf Fortress or Oxygen Not Included. Cave-ins and flowing sand wreck their plans in real time.
- **Headless gym for reinforcement learning** (large, bold): A Node build of the engine with no DOM exposes `reset(seed)`, `step(action)` and `observe()`, where the observation is the ASCII glyph grid plus telemetry. Train a lander autopilot offline with PPO and ship the weights with the game. *It builds on the roadmap's seeded RNG and the existing Glyphite.test step API.*
- **Jump-link navgraph for platformer enemies** (large, bold): Build a graph of walkable ledges automatically from `colTop` and solid runs, with walk, jump, drop and climb edges tested against each entity's real jump arc. The graph is patched locally when terrain changes.

- **Invent materials by describing them** (medium, crazy): 'A rock that burns blue and melts into glass' becomes a JSON `defineMaterial` spec from the LLM (toughness, glow, granular, fusing, vaporising). A local grammar handles common verbs, and free text goes through the Worker to Claude, which answers with narration plus engine calls from an allowed list (spawn, carve, light).

## Testing & debugging

- **CI flags stale thumbnails** (tiny, practical): CI renders each changed game's thumbnail recipe and fails, or posts a warning, when the result drifts too far from the committed thumb.png. The game list stops showing last month's look.
- **Hot-reload snapshot round-trip test** (tiny, practical): For each game, take the hot-reload snapshot mid-play, reload and restore it, step both copies 300 frames and compare state hashes. It catches snapshot() functions that forget state, which would also break save slots and search bots.
- **Input latency meter in the debug HUD** (tiny, practical): A debug overlay timestamps each input event against the first frame that reacts to it and shows the latency in ms and frames per device. It catches regressions when lighting moves to a worker.
- **NaN tripwire that names the first bad value** (tiny, practical): In debug builds, check body, particle and chunk fields for non-finite values after every subsystem step, then freeze and report which subsystem, object and field went bad first. A NaN stops silently spreading through the whole world.
- **Source maps so errors name the part** (tiny, practical): The build already knows where each page line came from (lineMap in tools/assemble.mjs); emit it as an inline source map and translate pageerror stacks in smoke.py, so errors read `engine/09_structures.js:84` instead of a line deep in the assembled page.
- **Stop hook that requires proof** (tiny, practical): A Stop hook won't let a session that touched engine/ or games/ finish without SYNTAX\_OK, a passing smoke test and screenshots newer than its last edit.
- **Test helper: step until a condition** (tiny, practical): `Glyphite.test.until(() => probe().enemyNear, 900)` steps up to 900 frames and returns how many it took, so behaviour tests read as 'the crawler reaches the player within 15 s'.
- **Time-to-first-frame budget per game** (tiny, practical): CI measures boot to first presented frame (atlas build, world generation, settleIslands) for each game and fails over budget, because a slow start is the first thing a phone player notices.
- **Two-tab local multiplayer via BroadcastChannel** (tiny, practical): A transport that links two tabs on the same machine with no server, so multiplayer games can be developed and smoke-tested offline and in CI.
- **Accessibility lint in the build** (small, practical): glyphite build warns when a HUD colour pair is below 4.5:1 contrast, an action has no touch or one-switch mapping, a touch button is smaller than about 44 px at phone width, or CALM is ignored.
- **AI and entity debug overlay** (small, practical): A debug key draws flow-field arrows as `< ^ > v`, paths as dotted `.`, vision cones as dim `'` cells and each entity's state name in the world grid, plus the director's tension curve and each NPC's goal and last LLM prompt and reply in the HUD. AI behaviour can then be tuned instead of guessed. *The debug view uses the same glyphs as the game, so it needs no extra drawing layer.*
- **Allocation-per-step budget test** (small, practical): Playwright samples the heap through the Chrome DevTools Protocol and measures bytes allocated per step over 600 steps of each game, failing above a budget. It catches stray closures and arrays that cause GC hitches on phones.
- **Common probe schema for every game** (small, practical): Standardise a few GAME.probe() fields (player position and velocity in metres, goal, hazards, score, lives, phase, won, lost). One bot library, one playtest report and one balancer then work on every game with no game-specific code.
- **Crash telemetry badge on the game list** (small, practical): window.onerror on play pages posts the stack, game version and renderer to D1. The game list shows a red badge on any game with recent errors, and the editor lists them.
- **Cross-browser determinism matrix** (small, practical): Playwright runs the same replay in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit and compares state hashes every 100 steps. Any Math function or sort order that diverges between engines gets flagged.
- **Debug build with bounds-checked typed arrays** (small, practical): `glyphite build --debug` wraps the engine's typed arrays so out-of-range writes (which JS silently drops) and NaN stores throw with a stack, and asserts invariants after every step. Release builds stay untouched.
- **Desync detector hashing the voxel grid** (small, practical): Every 60 ticks each peer hashes the grid, bodies and RNG state and sends the hash with its inputs. A mismatch flashes DESYNC in the HUD and saves both snapshots for comparison.
- **Differential tests for every accelerated path** (small, practical): Any fast path (GPU sand, WASM light, worker lighting) runs beside the JS reference on the same seed in CI and must match exactly, or within a tolerance written down in the test.
- **End-to-end test of the web editor** (small, practical): Playwright opens the editor, moves a tunable, edits a code part, waits for the in-browser rebuild and live preview, exports the CLAUDE tab's patch and checks that git apply reproduces the edit on a clean checkout. Today only the patch functions are unit-tested.
- **Engine code coverage from smoke runs** (small, practical): Collect V8 coverage with Playwright's page.coverage across every game's smoke and regression runs and map it back to engine part lines with the build's existing lineMap. Untested branches, such as a mount's onLost or chunk chipping, show up in a PR report.
- **Flash test in the smoke run** (small, practical): smoke.py records mean luminance across frames during a scripted explosion and fails on more than 3 large swings per second. Every game is checked for seizure risk in CI.
- **Frame-hitch chaos test** (small, practical): A smoke-test mode that injects rAF stalls of 50 to 2000 ms, tab hide and show, clock jumps and focus loss, then checks for tunnelling bodies, exploding velocities and NaN. It catches bugs players hit when they switch tabs.
- **Golden frames stored and diffed as text** (small, practical): Under the seeded RNG, store each game's world and HUD glyph grids with quantised colours per RES and renderer as .txt goldens, and diff them cell by cell in CI with readable output like 'row 31: `##` became `.:`' plus a heatmap of changed cells. Antialiasing and GPU differences cannot affect them, unlike pixel diffs. *Everything Glyphite draws is already a character grid, so goldens are exact, font-proof and tiny.*
- **In-game dev console in the HUD** (small, practical): A backtick console drawn in the HUD grid with commands that work in every game through the engine registries (spawn, tp, setmat, blast, give, slowmo, seed), and games can register their own.
- **Lighting G-buffer views, one term at a time** (small, practical): Toggle the preview to show only the sun shadow mask, AO, point lights, light soak, emissive or bloom, with normals drawn as `- | / \` and `< ^ > v` arrow glyphs. It is the ASCII version of a deferred renderer's debug views. *Even the debug views are text, so they work in both renderers for free.*
- **Lighting regression snapshots at fixed angles** (small, practical): Render a seeded scene at five sun angles and compare luminance histograms and shadow coverage with stored golden values, using tolerances that ignore random particles.
- **Map of where bots die** (small, practical): Run groups of bots and draw where they died or got stuck as a heat overlay on a full-level ASCII map. The map goes into the PR, so a balance change shows where it moved the difficulty.
- **Phone emulation smoke run with touch** (small, practical): Playwright with isMobile, hasTouch and a 390x844 viewport taps the on-screen buttons, checks that the HUD fits and no controls overlap, then repeats at 4x CPU throttle (CDP Emulation.setCPUThrottlingRate) to confirm the slow-device fallback kicks in.
- **Physics scenario tests drawn as ASCII** (small, practical): Regression tests are small ASCII drawings plus an expected outcome ('boulder above the roof: the roof breaks and the lamp ends on the ground', 'the U-tube settles level', 'the crate floats'), run at RES 1, 2 and 3 with pass/fail thresholds. Sessions write physics tests by drawing them instead of setting coordinates.
- **Readability linter at every detail level** (small, practical): Render every sprite, HUD screen and lighting preset at each RES and flag low-contrast HUD text, a player sprite that vanishes into nearby cells in the dark, sprites that blend into terrain, material glyph ramps too close to their neighbours, and text cut off on small screens.
- **Renderer parity check by glyph readback** (small, practical): Render the same frozen frame in WebGL and 2D, classify each cell's pixels back to the nearest atlas glyph and colour, and diff the two grids. One renderer drifting from the other shows up as exact cells, not a fuzzy pixel diff. *Visual changes must work in both renderers, and this checks it automatically.*
- **Renderer wipe compare: WebGL vs canvas** (small, practical): A draggable vertical wipe shows WebGL on one side and the 2D canvas fallback on the other, with a diff mode that colours cells whose glyph or colour disagree.
- **RES invariance checker for generators** (small, practical): Generate each seed at RES 1, 2 and 3 and compare metrics in metres (pad positions, cave connectivity, crystal count, open area) within tolerances. It catches generators that drift with detail.
- **RES-parity and determinism checker in CI** (small, practical): Run the same replay twice and at RES 1, 1.5, 2 and 3, compare trajectories, landing times and rest positions in metres, and report the first frame and subsystem that diverge beyond tolerance. It automates the rule that the world behaves the same at every detail level and catches the unscaled speeds sessions tend to add.
- **Soak test for leaks over long play** (small, practical): Run each game for 30 simulated minutes headless with random inputs while charting heap size, particle, chunk and mount counts and ms per step, and fail on steady growth. It finds leaks a 600-frame smoke test never sees.
- **Sprite readability score at every RES** (small, practical): Compare the glyph rendering's silhouette and edges with the source pixel art at each detail level and fail sprites that turn to mush. The pixel-art skill gets a number to improve.
- **Trajectory predictor overlay** (small, practical): Draw the player body's predicted path for the next 3 seconds with the current input held, as dotted glyphs that stop where it hits terrain. It helps tune thrust and gravity and could double as an easy-mode aid.
- **Tunable extremes sweep in CI** (small, practical): For every tunable in game.json, set it to its min, its max and random extreme combinations, run 600 steps at each RES, and fail on NaN, a pageerror or a stuck state. It keeps the rule of honest min and max values honest. *Tunables are what the user turns in the web editor, so no slider position should be able to crash a game.*
- **Watch expressions as ASCII sparklines** (small, practical): Type an expression like `lander.vy` or `particles.length` and get a scrolling sparkline drawn with `_.-'^` glyphs in a HUD corner. It catches oscillations and spikes without opening devtools.
- **World diff between two frames** (small, practical): Pick frames A and B and highlight every cell whose material changed, colour-coded as carved, deposited or fused. You see exactly what a blast or a sand slide did.
- **Cell inspector that explains any glyph** (medium, practical): Hold Z to magnify the cells under the cursor, or click one in a frozen frame, to see its char code, colour hex, material, heat, owning particle, why that glyph was picked, and a stacked bar of what lights it (sun, each named point light, ambient, AO, soak, emissive, bloom). You see exactly why a corner is too dark.
- **Clickable event timeline in the editor** (medium, practical): The engine's event chronicle (blast, detach, freeze, pickup, contact) scrolls as a log in the editor, and clicking an entry scrubs the replay to that exact frame and highlights the spot.
- **Conditional breakpoints on game events** (medium, practical): Freeze the preview when a condition first holds: a chunk detaches, heat passes 900 anywhere, a contact impulse passes X, or a custom expression turns true. The editor jumps to that frame.
- **One-key bug report with replay** (medium, practical): F8 freezes the game and files a GitHub issue through the Worker with the last 30 seconds of input, the tunables, console errors and a text frame, labelled so a Claude session can reproduce it exactly.
- **Per-subsystem perf budgets on every PR** (medium, practical): A bench harness steps fixed stress scenes (sand avalanche, 40 chunks, 32 shadowed lights) and each game for 600 frames at RES 3, timing sand, particles, bodies, light and render with performance.mark. CI posts the table against main on the PR and fails any subsystem more than 15 percent slower.
- **Physics overlay for contacts, velocities, rest timers** (medium, practical): Toggleable layers draw contact points as `*`, contact normals as `/|\`, velocity arrows, chunk rest timers and freeze countdowns in bright debug colours over the world. It targets bugs like the chunk resting on the lander that never freezes.
- **Property tests for physics invariants** (medium, practical): A small dependency-free property runner with shrinking checks random worlds at every RES: sand mass is conserved when nothing explodes, no body ends a step inside solid, resting bodies never gain energy, and every chunk freezes before its age limit.
- **Replay regression suite with state hashes** (medium, practical): Each game keeps golden replays with expected outcomes (landed, score, crystals) and FNV hashes of cells, bodies and probe() at checkpoints, re-simulated headless at RES 1-3 in CI. On a mismatch it prints the first diverging step and the subsystem whose state differs.
- **Seed playability validator with declared invariants** (medium, practical): game.json declares invariants (spawn free, exit reachable, every crystal minable, pad flat enough) that flood fill and A\* on occupancy check after each generation, so a failing seed is rejected or patched at runtime. CI runs 200 seeds per game and lists the broken ones.
- **Smoke test renders audio and a spectrogram** (medium, practical): smoke.py renders 10 seconds of the game's audio offline to shots/&lt;id&gt;.wav with a spectrogram PNG and RMS, peak and clipping numbers. Silence, clipping or a dead lane fails CI.
- **ASCII blue screen with Send to Claude** (small, bold): Catch exceptions per hook, freeze the game and print the stack trace in the HUD grid. A button copies a ready prompt for a Claude session with the error, game id, commit and replay.
- **Claims in descriptions are checked in CI** (small, bold): Sentences in game.json and NOTES.md get probe assertions, such as 'legs collapse above about 4 m/s', and CI checks them. Descriptions can't drift from how the game actually behaves.
- **Font swap stress test** (small, bold): Preview a game in other monospace fonts (IBM Plex Mono, Fira Mono, Courier) to check that glyph matching, the atlas and sprite readability survive a font change or a missing web font.
- **Glitch museum of preserved physics bugs** (small, bold): Before a funny bug gets fixed (a chunk launched into orbit, a lamp walking), its replay is saved against the build of the commit that has the bug. The portal shows these as a museum of playable exhibits, each linked to its fix PR.
- **Replay-driven bisect of engine regressions** (small, bold): `glyphite bisect <id> --replay crash.glyrec` runs git bisect over engine commits, rebuilding and replaying headless at each one, and names the commit that broke a landing. When an engine PR breaks another game's tests, CI runs it and a session proposes the fix in the same PR.
- **Bot playtesters with personas and reports** (medium, bold): A generic behaviour-tree bot plays each level hundreds of times headless as personas (cautious pilot, reckless pilot, explorer, button-masher, a first-timer who ignores the controls) and writes a report of deaths, soft-locks, out-of-bounds escapes and completion times. Claude reads it before tuning.
- **Fuzzer and curiosity agent for physics bugs** (medium, bold): A headless fuzzer and a novelty-seeking explorer throw random blasts, materials, tunables, gravity and detail switches at each game, stopping on any pageerror, NaN, body inside rock, energy blow-up, frame spike or chunk past its age limit. Each find is shrunk to a minimal seed and replay that becomes a repro test.
- **Material interaction matrix** (medium, bold): Run a tiny headless sim for every pair of materials, plus heat and blast, and show the outcome in a grid (fuses, vaporises, creeps into, nothing). Emergent combos and dead materials show at a glance.
- **Statistical RES parity with bot populations** (medium, bold): Where chaotic physics makes single trajectories drift apart, run the same trained policy at RES 1, 2 and 3 over 100 seeds and compare success rates and landing-speed spreads. A shift flags physics that quietly depends on the detail level.
- **Why did this fall? support-loss explainer** (medium, bold): Click a falling chunk to replay its support check, highlighting the path to the anchor it lost and the blast or chip that cut it. Collapses get a cause trace instead of guesswork.
- **Headless bot swarm for difficulty curves** (large, bold): Heuristic bots play thousands of seeds in Web Workers or a Cloudflare container and chart success rate against each tunable. Difficulty settings come from data instead of vibes.
- **Debugger that reopens any commit's exact build** (moonshot, bold): Store per-commit builds so a bug report reopens its exact build, frame and world in the editor, bisects in the browser against a replay and a pass condition, then hot-swaps today's code in to check whether it is fixed.
- **Mutation testing of the engine by Claude** (medium, crazy): A Claude session plants small bugs in the engine (a flipped sign in the contact impulse, a skipped support check) and checks that the test suite catches each one. Any bug the tests miss becomes a new test.

## Engine architecture & performance

- **API surface snapshot checked into git** (tiny, practical): The build writes api-surface.json listing every engine global, hook and signature, and CI requires any PR that changes it to also touch API.md and bump the version in engine.json.
- **Battery-aware detail and frame rate** (tiny, practical): Use the Battery Status API and the prefers-reduced-data media query to drop to RES 1 and 30 Hz lighting on low battery, with a toast explaining it and an override in settings.
- **Glyph atlas cached across visits** (tiny, practical): Store the built glyph atlas and sprite glyph-coverage tables in IndexedDB, keyed by font, cell size and device pixel ratio, so return visits skip the atlas build and reach the first frame sooner.
- **Glyph metadata table measured from the atlas** (tiny, practical): When the atlas is built, measure each glyph's ink coverage, centroid, bounding box and dominant stroke angle into a `GLYPH_META` table in the API. Ramps, sub-cell placement, streaks and the sprite matcher read it instead of hand-picked strings, and it stays correct if the font changes.
- **Lint that keeps the engine game-agnostic** (tiny, practical): CI collects every material, particle type and state name that games define and fails if any appears in engine/, enforcing the rule that the engine never names a game's things.
- **Memory report per detail level** (tiny, practical): probe() reports bytes per subsystem (the light arrays, render grids, particle pool) at the current RES and screen size, and the engine warns before a 4K screen at RES 3 passes a mobile memory budget.
- **Page weight budget per game** (tiny, practical): The build reports each page's bytes by part (engine parts, sprites, inlined MIDI, game code) and CI fails above a budget, so the one-file-per-game rule stays fast to load on phones.
- **Spatial hash for neighbour queries** (tiny, practical): A uniform-grid bucket index rebuilt each step, with `ents.near(x, y, r)` and `ents.inBox()`, shared by AI, projectiles, flocking and pickups. Every O(n²) proximity loop becomes a cheap lookup.
- **ASCII profiler bars in the HUD** (small, practical): A built-in key draws a stacked bar per frame for sand, particles, bodies, light sweep, render and audio as glyph bars in the HUD grid, with particle count, awake chunks and markers for blasts, detail switches and long frames. The same numbers go into probe() so a Claude session can see what is slow. *The HUD is already a text grid, so the profiler is drawn the same way as the game.*
- **Automatic quality ladder instead of a toast** (small, practical): Replace the one-time 'RUNNING SLOW' hint with a governor that watches perfAvg with hysteresis and steps down in order (bloom off, light every other frame, smaller particle budget, lower RES), climbing back when there is headroom.
- **Awake-region overlay for sand and light** (small, practical): Outline the world tiles the sand automaton is still stepping and the columns the light sweep recomputed this frame. You see why a scene is slow and when things fall asleep.
- **Base-95 encoding for inlined data** (small, practical): Sprites, MIDI and compressed blobs are inlined as base-95 strings that use exactly the 95 printable glyphs. They come out denser than base64 and keep the page within the printable-ASCII rule.
- **Cheat code registry with classic words** (small, practical): Glyphite.cheats.add('xyzzy', fn) works for words typed anywhere and for Konami-style key sequences, and using a cheat stamps a \~ on the score. The starter ships iddqd (invulnerable) and idkfa (full rockets).
- **Collision layers and masks** (small, practical): Bit masks per body, particle type and material decide what touches what. Ghosts pass through grains, player bullets ignore the player's own drone, and debris skips pickups.
- **Compact world saves with CompressionStream** (small, practical): Run-length encode the cell grid and pass it through the browser's built-in CompressionStream (deflate-raw), with no libraries. It gives save slots, and level links that fit in a URL fragment.
- **Deterministic math shim for cross-browser replays** (small, practical): Basic arithmetic and sqrt are exact under IEEE rules, but Math.sin, atan2, exp and hypot can differ between V8, SpiderMonkey and JavaScriptCore. Routing the simulation through the engine's own polynomial or table versions makes replays bit-identical in every browser.
- **Dirty-row texture uploads to the GPU** (small, practical): glTex re-uploads whole grid textures with texSubImage2D every frame; track the changed row range per texture and upload only that, skipping unchanged textures entirely. Calm scenes at RES 3 then send a fraction of the bytes to the GPU, which matters most on phones.
- **Engine API usage map per game** (small, practical): The build records which engine globals each game touches and draws a dependency map, so an engine change lists the games to smoke-test and dead engine APIs become visible.
- **Entity sleeping off-screen in big worlds** (small, practical): Entities far from the camera drop to a cheap abstract tick (position along their path, no physics) and wake into full bodies when they come near, so levels can hold thousands of creatures.
- **Event bus with a typed event chronicle** (small, practical): `on('door.red.open', fn)` and `emit()` connect switches, quests, music and cutscenes, while the engine itself emits typed events (blast, collapse, landing speed, pickup, body frozen) with tick and position into a ring buffer. Narrators, directors, achievements, the LLM and the editor's timeline all read the same log. *Many other ideas on this list need it as a foundation.*
- **Export traces to Perfetto** (small, practical): `Glyphite.trace(seconds)` records engine spans and events as Chrome trace JSON that opens in ui.perfetto.dev, so deep profiling needs no custom viewer.
- **Generation off the main thread** (small, practical): Run big world generation in a Web Worker, hand the cells back as a transferred ArrayBuffer and show an ASCII progress bar, so the page stays responsive at RES 3.
- **Guard engine state from accidental game writes** (small, practical): Parse game parts with a vendored acorn at build time and refuse assignments to engine-owned variables (W, H, RES, cells, cam and others) outside the documented setters. In one shared script scope a game can otherwise break the engine silently.
- **Idle the frame loop when the world sleeps** (small, practical): When no chunk is awake, no particles fly, the camera is still and no input arrives, skip texture uploads and present at about 10 fps until the next event. Title screens and pauses stop draining phone batteries.
- **Inline a 95-glyph JetBrains Mono subset** (small, practical): Embed a woff2 subset containing only the printable ASCII range as base64 in each page. That removes the only external request, the atlas rebuild after the font loads and font-version drift in golden tests, and lets pages work offline.
- **Interpolate rendering between fixed simulation steps** (small, practical): The loop draws the latest state after up to three fixed updates; keep each body's and the camera's previous transform and draw sprites, chunks and the view at the accumulator fraction. Motion becomes smooth on 120 and 144 Hz screens without touching the simulation.
- **Real-user performance telemetry by device** (small, practical): Play pages send perfAvg, renderer, RES, screen size and GPU string to Workers Analytics Engine; the game list shows median frame time per device class, and the default detail level comes from real data instead of a 640 px width test. *The Cloudflare Worker already fronts every page, so collection is one binding.*
- **Recover from WebGL context loss** (small, practical): Today the engine only calls preventDefault on webglcontextlost. It should rebuild shaders, atlases and textures on webglcontextrestored, because mobile Safari drops contexts in the background, and the smoke test should force a loss through WEBGL\_lose\_context.
- **Save files migrate across game versions** (small, practical): Saves record the game and engine version, and game.json declares small migration steps for renamed materials or tunables. An old cloud save then upgrades cleanly instead of loading garbage, and falls back to its seed if it can't be migrated.
- **Seeded RNG streams replace Math.random** (small, practical): Replace the engine's Math.random calls with Glyphite.rand split into named streams (world, gameplay, cosmetic, audio), so a dust puff never shifts where a blast chips rock, and Sfx randomness and the noise LFSR seed from the audio stream. Replays and test renders then look and sound identical.
- **Sim-time coroutines, timers and tweens** (small, practical): Add engine helpers such as wait(s), waitUntil(fn), every(s) and tween(obj,'x',to,0.5,'outBack') that advance on the fixed step. That makes them replayable, saveable and identical at every detail level, and it replaces hand-rolled countdown fields in game code.
- **Temporal light smoothing and half-rate updates** (small, practical): Blend the light buffers across frames and recompute far-off or static regions every other frame. It cuts cost at RES 3 and hides shimmer at shadow edges when chunks tumble.
- **Tree-shake engine parts using the clash table** (small, practical): The build already knows every engine top-level name for its clash check, so it can leave out whole parts a game never references (chip music, structures, sprites) from that game's page.
- **Two-stage generation: layout, then per-RES detail** (small, practical): Formal hooks genLayout(seed), which places pads, caves and routes in metres with no RES, and genDetail(layout, RES). Switching detail can then change texture but never gameplay.
- **Voice budget and priority for sound storms** (small, practical): Cap simultaneous Sfx voices, merge same-type requests within a frame into one louder event, and steal the quietest voice, so a 300-chunk collapse sounds huge instead of distorting.
- **Asset packs shared between games** (medium, practical): Bundle materials, particles, sprites and sounds from one game (say Regolith's regolith, He-3 crystal and lamp) into a pack another game imports with one line, with the build checking for name clashes.
- **Bitpacked solid mask for sweeps and queries** (medium, practical): Keep a one-bit-per-cell solid mask in Uint32Array rows next to cells, so the sun sweep, support flood, raycasts and body contacts skip 32 cells at a time with Math.clz32. It is also the natural occupancy texture for GPU lighting.
- **Dirty-tile sleeping for every simulation** (medium, practical): Track activity per 32x32 tile across sand, liquid, heat, fire and gas so idle regions cost nothing and big worlds stay fast.
- **Flood-fill light levels for many lights** (medium, practical): A cheap Minecraft-style light propagation (a BFS that decays per cell, updated only around edits) handles hundreds of torches and glowing cells, while ray-cast shadows stay reserved for a few hero lights.
- **Incremental lighting from changed regions** (medium, practical): Recompute sun and AO only in columns and chunks whose cells changed, using the existing LSTAMP and awake map. Static point-light contributions stay cached until something inside their radius moves.
- **Lighting worker via COOP/COEP cross-origin isolation** (medium, practical): Have the Cloudflare Worker send Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy and COEP: credentialless headers so pages can use SharedArrayBuffer, and a worker computes frame N+1's light from the shared cells while the main thread renders frame N. *The Worker already fronts every page, so isolation is a header change, and credentialless keeps the Google Fonts stylesheet loading.*
- **Named scalar field layers** (medium, practical): Generalise the heat field into `defineField('oxygen', {diffuse, decay, blockedBy})` with sources, sinks and an overlay renderer. One mechanism then covers gases, pheromones, scent, noise, power and pollution.
- **One arena buffer for all world state** (medium, practical): Allocate cells, heat, light, particles and render grids as views into one ArrayBuffer sized for the largest RES. Detail switches stop churning the GC, and a full save state becomes a single slice().
- **Opt-in kits and plugins via game.json** (medium, practical): Kits and plugins live in engine/plugins/&lt;name&gt;/ with a plugin.json naming their parts, engine version range, step phase and the grids they read and write, and a game opts in with `"uses": ["kit/character", "plugin/water"]`. The core stays small and each page carries only what it uses.
- **Pin each game to an engine version** (medium, practical): game.json names its engine version ("engine": "0.2.0") and the build assembles the game from that tagged engine via git show, so upgrading becomes a deliberate PR that reruns the game's replays. An engine change can no longer quietly break an old game.
- **Sun sweep and AO in a fragment shader** (medium, practical): Upload cells as an R8 occupancy texture and compute sun shadows per pixel by marching toward the sun, with ambient occlusion from a mip chain of that texture instead of the CPU summed-area table. A jump-flood distance field built from the same texture gives point lights sphere-traced soft shadows, so the CPU only simulates.
- **Pick glyphs on the GPU** (large, practical): Upload material, normal and light textures and let the fragment shader choose ramp and slope glyphs per cell, leaving JS with only the simulation; the 2D path keeps the JS scene shader. This frees the main thread at RES 3.
- **Amortised point-light shadows across frames** (small, bold): Re-trace a quarter of the shadowed point lights each frame and blend toward the result, so 64 lamps cost about as much as 16. Fast movers and flashes keep updating every frame.
- **Cheap agent crowds in typed arrays** (medium, bold): A `defineAgent` system steps thousands of single-cell walkers in typed arrays the way the sand automaton works (walk, step up, fall, climb) and runs JS state machines only on events. It would power lemmings, ants, colonists and zombie hordes.
- **Engine parts as real ES modules** (medium, bold): Turn 01\_core through 11\_loop into ES modules with explicit imports and exports for a real dependency graph, per-module tests and go-to-definition. The build still flattens them into one script scope, so games keep the short global API.
- **Incremental support checks after blasts** (medium, bold): Instead of flooding from the blast area toward the anchors after every blast, keep connected-component labels per structure and repair them only along the cut, falling back to a full flood when unsure. Blasting a huge cathedral then costs about as much as blasting a lamp post.
- **Per-system step rates and substeps** (medium, bold): Let each system run at its own rate (heat at 10 Hz, sand at 30 Hz, bodies at 240 Hz with substeps), cutting cost where nobody can see the difference and stiffening contacts where it matters.
- **Struct-of-arrays entity store for game objects** (medium, bold): Generalise the particle pool's SoA typed arrays into defineComponent and query, so a game with 500 enemies or bullets stays allocation-free and the whole store snapshots with one copy.
- **Terminal renderer as a third backend** (medium, bold): A `renderANSI()` path next to WebGL and 2D returns the frame as an ANSI string, so `node tools/glyphite.mjs play <id>` runs any game in a terminal with no browser. It is the base for SSH play, text snapshots in CI and asciinema export. *Every frame is already a grid of ASCII cells, so a terminal is a native output.*
- **Falling sand as a WebGPU compute shader** (large, bold): Keep cells and heat on the GPU and run the sand, liquid, heat and fire automata as Margolus-block passes (2x2 blocks with alternating offsets, so threads never write the same cell), as WebGPU compute or WebGL fragment shaders over ping-pong textures, with the CPU path as fallback. Full-screen sandboxes at RES 3, around a million cells at 60 fps, become possible. *Sand is Glyphite's signature system, and a block automaton maps cleanly onto compute shaders.*
- **GPU particles with delayed terrain readback** (large, bold): Move sparks, smoke and grains to WebGL2 transform feedback or WebGPU compute, colliding against the occupancy texture, and read back only grains that came to rest a frame later (fence plus async readback) to turn them into terrain. Around 100k particles on desktop.
- **Noita-style four-pass multithreaded sand** (large, bold): Split the world into 64x64 chunks and update them on a pool of workers in four checkerboard passes, so neighbouring chunks never run at once; the existing awake map lets sleeping chunks be skipped entirely.
- **Sandboxed mod scripts in QuickJS-WASM** (large, bold): Remixes and community content run their scripts in a QuickJS-in-WASM sandbox with a small capability API (spawn, paint, sound) and no access to the DOM, network or cookies. Untrusted player mods become safe to share.
- **System scheduler with declared grid access** (large, bold): Replace the hard-coded update order with registered systems that declare which grids they read and write (cells, heat, dyn, light). The scheduler orders and times them and knows which can safely move to a worker.
- **WASM SIMD kernels inlined as base64** (large, bold): Write the sand step and sun sweep as WASM SIMD (hand-written WAT, or AssemblyScript compiled at build time) inlined as base64 to keep the one-file rule, with the JS versions as fallback and reference.
- **WebGPU as a third renderer** (large, bold): Add a WebGPU path ahead of WebGL and 2D in the fallback chain: instanced glyph quads, with the sun sweep, point-light shadows, glyph picking and bloom as compute passes over storage textures, and timestamp queries feeding the profiler. The smoke test covers all three renderers.
- **Fixed-point Q16.16 core for bodies and chunks** (large, crazy): Store rigid-body and chunk positions and velocities as Int32 fixed point aligned to the grid, so contacts against voxels are exact and the simulation runs identically in any browser or on a server.
- **Moon@home: visitors' browsers simulate shards** (moonshot, crazy): A huge persistent moon is split into tiles, and each visitor's idle tab simulates one tile and sends diffs to a Durable Object. Two browsers run the same tile so their hashes can be cross-checked.

## Multiplayer & social

- **Pass-the-phone hot-seat turns** (tiny, practical): An engine helper for local turn-based play: a 'PASS TO PLAYER 2' ASCII card that hides the screen between turns, per-player scores and a turn timer.
- **Live presence on the game list** (small, practical): A Durable Object tracks who is playing what by Access identity, so the launcher shows 'sebastian is in Regolith Lander: spectate / join' next to each game.
- **Moderation pass for shared levels and messages** (small, practical): Community levels, carved messages and NPC chat logs pass through a Workers AI or Claude classifier before others see them, with a report key and a review queue in the portal.
- **Player profile with ASCII medal badges** (small, practical): A per-Access-user page with playtime, personal bests and badges earned from engine events (first 1000-cell collapse, landed on one leg), each drawn as a small glyph medal.
- **Players' wishes feed the roadmap** (small, practical): Each play page has a one-line wish box for friends behind Access, stored in D1. A weekly session sorts the wishes into ROADMAP items or small PRs.
- **Push alerts when your record falls** (small, practical): The installed PWA subscribes to Web Push, and when someone beats your time the Worker sends 'Your Regolith record fell by 0.4 s - race their ghost'.
- **Tunable preset gallery with votes** (small, practical): Save a named slider set such as 'Low-G Chaos' to D1 from the editor; others apply it in one click and upvote it, and the top presets appear under each game. *Turns the editor's tunables into something friends trade.*
- **Daily seeded run with a friends leaderboard** (medium, practical): A Worker cron picks each day's seed from the UTC date and stores it in KV, with an optional LLM-written briefing and lore line. Scores and their replays go into D1 under the Access email, and the title screen shows the board as an ASCII table where you can watch the best run. *Worker, KV and D1 sit behind the Access login already, so the board is friends-only by default.*
- **Ghost racers from recorded inputs** (medium, practical): Using the seeded RNG, record each frame's input and replay the best run as a non-colliding sprite drawn at about 30% brightness that casts no light. The best ghost per track is stored in KV, so you can race your own past run or the leader's.
- **Leaderboards per tunable fingerprint** (medium, practical): A D1 table keyed by game, version and a hash of tunable values: default settings get the real board, and every modded slider set automatically gets its own. *Tunables are a core editor feature, so scores must say which physics they were set under.*
- **Peer-to-peer WebRTC with Durable Object signalling** (medium, practical): A Durable Object only does matchmaking and the SDP exchange; inputs then travel over WebRTC data channels directly between players, with Cloudflare Realtime TURN as the fallback, for lower latency than relaying.
- **Record shareable gameplay clips with sound** (medium, practical): MediaRecorder captures the canvas stream and the chip audio bus into a WebM clip with an ASCII title card. Recording starts with a key, or automatically after a big collapse.
- **ASCII emote bursts in multiplayer** (tiny, bold): Number keys release short-lived emote particles such as '&lt;3', 'gg' or '!!' that drift up from your ship, built on the particle registry and sent to everyone in the room.
- **Shared sky clock across all players** (small, bold): A Durable Object holds one world time and weather timeline, so everyone on the site sees the same dusk or the same storm at the same moment.
- **Async turn-based matches on Durable Objects** (medium, bold): Turn-based kits (artillery, tactics, chess-likes) send each turn's inputs to a Durable Object, and the deterministic simulation replays them on each client. Friends can play one match over several days, behind the Access login. *Turn-based play plus a seeded RNG needs no real-time netcode.*
- **Bomb-defusal co-op with a printed manual** (medium, bold): One player works the device in the game and the other reads a procedurally generated ASCII manual, printable or on a phone. Each seed changes the wiring rules, and a wrong cut blows up real terrain.
- **Crew co-op: one flies, one runs the ship** (medium, bold): Two players crew one lander. The pilot steers while the engineer splits power between thrust, lamps and legs and patches hull cells as they crack. Each player sees a different HUD on their own device.
- **Exquisite-corpse relay levels** (medium, bold): Each friend sees only the last screen of a shared level and paints the next one; the chain is stored in D1, and the whole level is revealed and playable once eight people have contributed.
- **Friends' crash wreckage you can replay** (medium, bold): Each crash stores its position and the last 5 seconds of input. Other players find frozen chunk debris where friends died, and touching it replays that crash as a ghost.
- **Messages carved into other players' worlds** (medium, bold): Where you crashed you can carve a short message into the rock, as in Dark Souls, stored in D1 by game, level seed and coordinates. Other players see faint beacon glyphs and read them by touching them; messages are built from a word list, with an edge moderation model for free text.
- **Phone as gamepad via ASCII QR code** (medium, bold): The big screen shows a QR code drawn in '#' glyphs; scanning it opens a controller page joined to the same Durable Object room, with touch thrust buttons and DeviceOrientation tilt for steering. A whole family can steer landers from their phones. *A QR code is just a grid of glyphs, and Durable Objects suit relaying controller input.*
- **Spectate by streaming glyph diffs** (medium, bold): The player's page sends each frame's changed glyph and colour cells, delta-compressed to a few hundred bytes, to a Durable Object that fans them out to any number of viewers. Spectators only paint the grid and never run the simulation. *A text frame is a few KB and a frame diff far less, much cheaper than video.*
- **Spectator auto-director camera** (medium, bold): For spectators, a director picks the most interesting player from engine events (blast size, speed, near misses), cuts or pans between players, and replays big explosions in slow motion.
- **Tethered co-op: two landers, one rope** (medium, bold): Two players' craft are joined by a rope of spring-linked rigid segments and must carry a crystal cargo between them, either on one keyboard or online. Every thrust tugs your partner, so co-op comes straight out of the physics.
- **Voice chat in co-op rooms via Cloudflare Realtime** (medium, bold): An SFU-backed voice channel for co-op and versus rooms, with an optional bitcrushed 2A03-style radio filter and push-to-talk that keys a chip squelch.
- **Weekly async duel brackets** (medium, bold): A Worker cron pairs friends each week; both play the same seed whenever they like, the better replay advances, and the bracket is drawn on the portal in `+-|` box art with each match's replays one click away.
- **Classroom mode with a teacher dashboard** (large, bold): A teacher opens a room with a fixed game, seed and locked tunables; students join by code, and a Durable Object streams each student's probe values to a live dashboard. The teacher can push a new tunable to everyone at once. *Durable Objects and the tunables system already exist, which is most of a physics lab for 30 students.*
- **Local split screen that merges when close** (large, bold): Engine support for two to four cameras over one shared simulated world, each drawing into its own region of the character grid with WASD and arrow-key layouts, for racing and versus artillery. When two players are close they share one view, and when they drift apart the screen splits Voronoi-style along the line between them, with the seam drawn as a bright line of `/` or `\` glyphs.
- **Lockstep co-op rooms on Durable Objects** (large, bold): One Durable Object per room relays inputs over WebSockets with deterministic lockstep (seeded RNG, fixed tick, about three ticks of input delay). Two landers share one destructible moon with no simulation on the server.
- **Replay-verified high scores in a Worker** (large, bold): A headless engine build re-simulates each submitted input log inside a Worker or Durable Object and accepts the score only if the sim reaches the same result, so leaderboards are cheat-proof without trusting the client. *The engine is dependency-free JS on typed arrays, so the same code runs at the edge.*
- **Spectate playtests as input streams** (large, bold): Because the sim is deterministic, watching a friend playtest a draft streams only their keys plus the seed through a Durable Object. Your browser re-simulates the run, which costs bytes instead of video.
- **Chat plays: viewers vote events in** (medium, crazy): Spectators in a room, or Twitch chat read over its IRC WebSocket, vote on events the game exposes (`!thrust`, a meteor, sun on or off, sand rain) in 250 ms windows (democracy) or apply every command (anarchy). Points can be spent to drop boulders, turning watching into chaotic interference.
- **Asymmetric god-mode: one player is the moon** (large, crazy): One player flies the lander while a second, on another device in the same Durable Object room, clicks to drop meteors, swing the sun or crack the ground from an energy budget. Destruction and light become the opponent's weapons. *Uses the engine's existing blasts, sun and structure collapse as player-controlled verbs.*
- **Discord Activity for group play** (large, crazy): Package the launcher as a Discord Activity so a voice channel can open a game together, with the Durable Object room tied to that channel.
- **Glyphite Multiverse: items travel between games** (large, crazy): A Durable Object holds each player's inventory across games, so a He-3 crystal mined in Regolith turns up as a fuel cell in Cave Drone, and a `portal` material sends your sprite and cargo to another game's page. The engine side is a small `Glyphite.universe` get/put API, and each game declares which items it accepts.
- **Named NPCs that remember every player** (large, crazy): Each named character is a Durable Object holding a rolling memory summary shared by every player behind the login. The miner remembers that your sister blew up his house yesterday and gossips about it today. *Access means a known circle of players, so shared NPC memory feels personal rather than risky.*

You log in to find what friends blew up overnight, and over weeks the moon is scarred by everyone who played. *Durable Objects map naturally onto a chunked, destructible world.*

## Input & accessibility

- **Hold-to-toggle and timing assists** (tiny, practical): A setting turns any hold action into a toggle (tap to start the burn, tap to stop) and lengthens double-tap and hold windows. It helps players with tremor or limited grip.
- **Interaction prompts near usable things** (tiny, practical): interactable(ent, '\[E\] open', fn) shows the nearest prompt above the object through worldToHud, and adds a touch button automatically on phones.
- **Left-handed and resizable touch layouts** (tiny, practical): Settings to mirror the touch button rows, scale them, change their opacity or drag them into place, saved per game. Players are not stuck with one fixed layout on every phone.
- **On-screen input display as ASCII keycaps** (tiny, practical): An optional HUD strip shows the keys, buttons and stick positions in use as keycaps (\[W\] lit, \[A\] dim, stick ( o )). It helps streams, tutorials and bug reports, and replays can show their inputs next to the run.
- **Camera comfort settings against motion sickness** (small, practical): Settings for camera lag, look-ahead and dead zone, separate switches for parallax sky scroll and screen shake, and a cap on how fast the camera may move during blasts. They go further than CALM for players who get motion sick.
- **Colour-blind modes as a shader colour matrix** (small, practical): A 3x3 colour matrix in both the WebGL glyph shader and the 2D fallback simulates protan, deutan and tritan vision for checking, or daltonizes the frame to push confusable material and HUD colours apart. HUD lamps also get a letter or shape (\[OK\], \[!!\]), so HC.green and HC.red never carry meaning by hue alone.
- **Controller-aware button prompts** (small, practical): Prompts and the controls card show \[A\] or \[Cross\], a key name, or 'tap', depending on the device used last, all from one action table. The on-screen touch buttons hide as soon as a gamepad is used.
- **Floating ASCII thumbsticks for touch** (small, practical): A virtual stick appears wherever a thumb lands, drawn on the HUD as glyphs like ( o ), and sends analog values to the action layer; two sticks make twin-stick games playable on phones. They replace rows of fixed \[ &lt; \] \[ &gt; \] buttons.
- **Gyro aim from controllers over WebHID** (small, practical): Read the DualSense or Switch Pro gyro through WebHID and map it to aim or lander tilt, with a ratchet button and sensitivity tunables. It gives precise analog control without sticks.
- **Haptics from blasts, contacts and thrust** (small, practical): Explosion power, contact impulse and Sfx.engine throttle drive Gamepad dual-rumble and navigator.vibrate patterns: a hard landing thumps the heavy motor and rocket hiss buzzes the light one. Phone players feel the blasts too. *The physics already knows every impulse, so the haptics come almost for free.*
- **High-contrast minimum-light mode** (small, practical): A setting raises the light floor and brightens the edges between solid and air. Terrain stays readable in dark caves and night levels, and shadows keep their shape.
- **HUD scale separate from world detail** (small, practical): A player setting, remembered across games, runs the HUD at 1.5x or 2x cell size (the HUD already has its own hcols and hrows) and reflows it for portrait phones while the world stays at RES 3. An optional HUD-only typeface such as Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono, with distinct b/d/p/q shapes and wider spacing, helps low-vision and dyslexic players without touching the world atlas.
- **Input buffering and coyote time in actions** (small, practical): The action layer remembers a press for a tunable 80-150 ms and still accepts it slightly after a ledge edge or a cooldown, so jumps and shots happen when the player meant them. It also makes play fairer for people with slower reactions.
- **Keyboard-navigable portal and editor** (small, practical): Visible focus rings, skip links, arrow-key navigation of the game list, and shortcuts for every editor action with a '?' help overlay, so the whole site works without a mouse. The editor's sliders get aria-valuetext with units.
- **Mirror the HUD into a screen-reader live region** (small, practical): Copy the HUD grid into a visually hidden &lt;pre&gt;, plus an aria-live region that announces changed rows and key events such as 'FUEL 12% low' or 'ALT 42 m'. Screen readers then read the game's own words, with nothing extra to write. *The HUD is already a grid of ASCII text, so it is close to accessible out of the box.*
- **Photosensitivity limiter for flashes and bloom** (small, practical): Cap how much screen luminance can change per frame from blast flashes, bloom and shock rings, and allow at most 3 bright flashes per second (WCAG 2.3.1). It extends the existing CALM reduced-motion flag.
- **Shape-coded materials so colour is never alone** (small, practical): Materials, particles and pickups declare an alternate glyph set for an accessibility mode, so hot rock, crystal and pad metal differ by shape (#, \*, =) as well as by hue. A build lint warns when two important materials share a glyph and have similar colours. *The engine already picks a glyph for every cell, so shape can carry meaning at no extra cost.*
- **Sub-frame input timestamps** (small, practical): Record each keydown's event.timeStamp and apply thrust for the exact fraction of the step it was held, rather than rounding to whole frames. Frame-perfect tricks become consistent across 60 and 144 Hz screens. *Speedrunners need inputs to behave the same on every machine.*
- **Touch gestures for engine controls** (small, practical): Built-in gestures that work in every game: pinch to change the detail level, two-finger tap to pause, long-press a cell to inspect it, drag to aim. They sit alongside the - + G M P keys.
- **Trained autopilot as an assist mode** (small, practical): An RL or PID autopilot holds a hover or cancels drift while the player only steers, with a slider from full assist to off. New players, and players with limited motor control, can still land.
- **Named input actions across keys, pads and touch** (medium, practical): Games declare actions in game.json and read Input.action('thrust') as a 0..1 value, or input.move and input.aim as vectors, fed alike by the keyboard, Gamepad API buttons and sticks, and touch. An analog trigger then gives Regolith Lander a real variable throttle, and twin-stick games work the same on every device. *Every game inherits this one input layer, and remapping, assists, replays and prompts can all build on it.*
- **Rebinding screen drawn in the HUD grid** (medium, practical): A press-to-bind controls screen built from hudBox and hudText lists every declared action with its keys, pad buttons and touch slot, and saves the bindings in localStorage under the game id. Duplicate bindings are flagged in red.
- **Analog thrust from pen pressure** (tiny, bold): PointerEvent.pressure from a stylus or pressure-sensitive trackpad maps to an analog action, so pressing harder burns harder. It is a one-line extension of the action layer.
- **Anti-shimmer glyph hysteresis** (small, bold): A cell keeps its glyph until another glyph is a clearly better match, so slowly moving light and rotating sprites stop flickering between near-equal characters. A slider sets how sticky glyphs are, which helps photosensitive and easily distracted players. *Shimmer between best-match glyphs is a problem only a text renderer has.*
- **DIY controllers over WebSerial and WebHID** (small, bold): A tiny serial protocol so an Arduino or Pico with a throttle lever and a big red button can fly the lander, plus a Stream Deck profile that shows live ASCII icons (fuel, legs, lamp) on its keys.
- **Look mode: inspect any cell** (small, bold): A roguelike 'look' cursor moved by keyboard, a mouse hover or a long-press shows and announces what a cell is, read from the material and particle registries ('basalt, toughness 3, 140 C', 'He-3 crystal'). Blind players can explore cell by cell, and newcomers learn what each glyph means.
- **MIDI knobs and pads as game controls** (small, bold): Extend the existing Web MIDI input so control-change knobs and faders drive analog actions (a fader for throttle) and drum pads trigger one-shot actions. To bind a control, select an action on the rebinding screen and wiggle a knob (MIDI learn). *Web MIDI is already wired up for the chip synth's keyboard.*
- **Tactile map export for swell paper** (small, bold): Export a level as a high-contrast black-and-white SVG sized for swell paper or a braille embosser, with materials as distinct textures and a braille legend. A blind player can feel the cave before flying it.
- **Tilt the phone to steer** (small, bold): DeviceOrientation gamma maps to the lander's roll and beta to throttle, after a 'hold flat and tap to calibrate' step, with dead-zone and sensitivity tunables. On iOS the permission prompt comes after the start tap.
- **Accessible DOM text renderer** (medium, bold): A third renderer writes the HUD, and optionally the world, as real text spans in a &lt;pre&gt;. Screen readers can read it, and the browser can select, zoom and translate it.
- **Localisation with ASCII transliteration** (medium, bold): Strings move into game.json tables read with t('key'). Claude translates them at build time (Workers AI handles NPC lines), and the build transliterates the result to codes 32-126: ae for æ, ss for ß, romaji for Japanese. *The ASCII-only rule turns localisation into a fun constraint, not a font problem.*
- **One-switch scanning input mode** (medium, bold): Games declare a one-button scheme: either the HUD highlights actions in turn and the single switch fires the highlighted one, or a game-specific scheme such as 'tap to burn while the lander auto-levels'. CI checks that each game can still be finished this way.
- **Scene narrator built from the material grid** (medium, bold): On a key press or a timer, an aria-live line describes the surroundings from the material and entity grids ('pad 40 m right, slope rising left, falling 3 m/s'), and a GAME.describe() hook lets a game write its own. The smoke test fails if the description is ever empty. *Every cell of the frame already means something, so a description is cheap and accurate.*
- **Voice commands for hands-free play** (medium, bold): Hold a key and say a short word the game declares ('burn', 'left', 'pause'). Browser SpeechRecognition or Whisper (on Workers AI, or the hornbaek.org home server) turns it into an action, a HUD line shows the transcript, and the same path can dictate tunable changes in the editor. *It can reuse the two-engine speech setup (browser recogniser or Whisper) that is already built for the other hornbaek.org sites.*
- **Ambient light sensor picks the theme** (tiny, crazy): Where AmbientLightSensor or a camera brightness estimate is available, switch to the high-contrast phosphor theme outdoors and dim the bloom at night.
- **Blow into the mic to thrust** (small, crazy): A breath-controller mode: microphone loudness drives the throttle and whistle pitch steers, both read through an AnalyserNode on the existing Web Audio graph.
- **E-ink and low-refresh display mode** (small, crazy): Draw black on white, turn off busy effects, and update only the changed cells a few times a second. Slow-paced games and puzzles then work in e-reader browsers.
- **Heart-rate strap drives tension and HUD pulse** (small, crazy): A Web Bluetooth heart-rate monitor feeds a pulse into the HUD, the tension director and the music's intensity layers. A calm heartbeat on landing scores a bonus.
- **Aim with your eyes via webcam gaze** (medium, crazy): Webcam gaze estimation (WebGazer-style), calibrated by looking at glyph targets, drives mouse aim, while keys or a single switch fire. It is a new input for players who cannot use a mouse.
- **Blink-only enemies through the webcam** (medium, crazy): Face landmark detection notices when you blink, and statue enemies move only while your eyes are closed, like the Weeping Angels. A keyboard fallback stands in for blinking.
- **Steer by head tilt with FaceDetector** (medium, crazy): Where the browser's Shape Detection API is available, eye landmarks from the webcam give head roll and nod, mapped to steering and throttle through the action layer. This is a hands-free control scheme that needs no downloaded model.
- **Adaptive triggers through WebHID** (large, crazy): On a DualSense, WebHID output reports stiffen the right trigger as fuel runs low and make it pulse when the engine stutters, while the controller's gyro gives fine aim. Players feel the rocket in their finger.
- **Refreshable braille terrain line** (large, crazy): Encode a 40-cell terrain profile around the player as 8-dot braille in an aria-live row. A braille display then shows the shape of the ground under the player's fingers during flight. *The world is already a grid of characters, so braille output is just a resampling of it.*
- **Fully blind-playable Regolith Lander** (moonshot, crazy): Combine the scene narrator, chip sonar, sound captions, the braille terrain line and assists, and prove the result with a CI bot that lands using only the accessibility outputs. Then publish what worked as an engine accessibility checklist.
